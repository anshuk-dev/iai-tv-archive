# AI is a tool, not an agent

*The challenge will be to keep it that way*

**By Narain D. Batra** | *11th October 2024* | *Read time: approx. 10 mins* | *2,032 words*

![AI is a tool, not an agent](https://iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWyI4MDAiLCI1MDAiXQ/ai-is-a-tool-and-not-an-agent.webp)

---

*Elon Musk put the probability that Artificial Superintelligence will end humanity at 20%. Superintelligence promises to change everything from politics, to health, from consciousness to what it means to be human. Narain Batra here argues that Artificial Superintelligence is now inevitable. We must learn to harness it, before it’s too late.*

Every society has a set of core values which give it a unique form and shape, a distinctive character. For example, the First Amendment has given American society the character of openness – an environment of randomness, a large zone for error making and creative fearlessness, possibilities for chance discoveries – all of which has turned America into a nation of tinkerers and innovators. In this open dynamic system, a system in a constant state of equilibrium-disequilibrium, turbulence-harmony, there’s no endgame or finality. Out of this open dynamic system arose the Internet, social media, artificial intelligence, and now generative AI, said to be the most transformative technology that would eventually lead to the development of artificial general intelligence, or AGI – a form of superintelligence surpassing human intelligence, simultaneously feeding widespread exuberance and catastrophic fears that it might lead to the end of humanity.

\_\_\_

The rise of AGI, singularity, or superintelligence is inevitable

\_\_\_

An open dynamic system based on freedom reinforced with a competitive marketplace and a magnet for global talent, such as the United States and Europe, should respond to the challenge of generative AI and its subsequent evolutionary forms by incorporating it into its fabric, its core values, its own DNA. As with previous technologies of freedom such as the printing press, the Internet and social media, generative AI will enable more people to access advanced informational capabilities, empowering individuals and groups to be more creative and  productive. By lowering the threshold of accessing knowledge through instant translation in myriad languages, generative AI will raise our intelligence and make societies more productive.  By automating certain cognitive tasks, it can free up mental capacities for people to focus on higher-level thinking and decision-making. Generative AI might currently be domesticated in the form of services and assistants, but this won’t be the end state of the technology, and new models will emerge.

The rise of AGI, singularity, or superintelligence is inevitable because generative AI systems such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Gemini among others have the potential to evolve. This may happen through [capability overhang](https://www.theverge.com/2022/12/8/23499728/ai-capability-accessibility-chatgpt-stable-diffusion-commercialization), the ability of a Gen.AI model to uncover and develop more knowledge from its current knowledge bases; [recursive self-improvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recursive_self-improvement), the ability of a Gen.AI  model to train itself by collecting data on its own and keep learning and improving; and [multi-agent systems](https://www.turing.ac.uk/research/interest-groups/multi-agent-systems) coordinating to create [combinatorial innovations](https://www.newthingsunderthesun.com/pub/8f77puuw/release/3), emergent combinations of knowledge or tools that provide new capabilities.

Through the establishment of dynamic guardrails and alignment with human ethical values, superintelligence under human control can solve complex problems that are currently beyond human comprehension – for example, predicting the lifespan of a pandemic, identifying the mechanisms of complex neurological dysfunctions, and coming up with solutions to hitherto intractable geopolitical problems. Just imagine: if we had superintelligence capabilities, it might have been possible to foresee and predict the rise and global spread of Covid-19. Superintelligence might have helped Israel to foresee the Hamas 7 October attack, which could have been preempted and saved thousands of innocent lives – or it might have given Israel an alternative route to eliminate Hamas instead of brutalizing the entire Palestinian people.

Superintelligence could open new doors, reveal alternative futures, and thus increase boundaries of human freedom. Today generative AI systems are trained on currently available massive datasets of text and code.  But if generative AI begins to use real world observations and tools such as apps on its own, its capabilities will grow exponentially. Apps are pervasive. We use them all the time – for example, when we order a rideshare, send a mobile payment through PayPal, change the thermostat temperature from our phone, order a pizza, we’re using an app created by Application Programming Interface (API). The tools-using generative AI would be no different from tools-using humans except that generative AI would be much faster than us and potentially free from any ethical constraints.  Bruce Schneier and Nathan Sanders of Harvard University [argued](https://www.wired.com/story/does-chatgpt-make-you-nervous-try-chatgpt-with-a-hammer/)that tools-using generative AI ‘could improve its own capabilities, using tools to ask questions, conduct research, and write code to incorporate into itself.’ And it would keep learning, improving and growing.

Could such a system become autonomous, develop self-awareness, do things on its own? If so, it won’t take long to build Artificial General Intelligence – perhaps eventually resulting in the Singularity, a system smarter than humans that keeps evolving into superintelligence. Could we domesticate AGI or pull the plug and disable it, if necessary?

\_\_\_

Generative AI and its evolution is unstoppable

\_\_\_

Comparing AI tools-use to human behavior is misleading. Humans have inherent goals, values, and motivations that guide their use of tools. Current GenAI lacks such intrinsic drives, and its tools-use would likely be more akin to pattern recognition and manipulation than conscious, goal-oriented action. The assertion that pulling the plug would be simple, however, overlooks the potential challenges of controlling complex, rapidly evolving AI systems. Disabling critical functions might have unintended consequences for systems that rely on them, and alternative control mechanisms may be necessary. The challenge is to keep AI systems under our control and use them as our collaborators, as our smarter second selves.

Generative AI and its evolution is unstoppable. The rush for the goldmine by enterprises all over the world has created a sense of urgency to build legal, ethical, and technological guardrails for evolving AI so that society does not lose control over this emerging transformative technology. In May 2023, 350 AI experts and public figures expressed their concern about AI risk and issued a [brief statement](https://www.safe.ai/statement-on-ai-risk#open-letter): “Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war.”  Although this dystopian fear seemed an overreaction, the message has gone home and has sparked important conversations about the potential dangers of inaction, which has led to some definitive regulatory steps.

[The Biden AI Executive Order on Artificial Intelligence](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2023/10/30/fact-sheet-president-biden-issues-executive-order-on-safe-secure-and-trustworthy-artificial-intelligence/) and [the EU AI Act](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/topics/en/article/20230601STO93804/eu-ai-act-first-regulation-on-artificial-intelligence) are major strides in regulating the emerging AI, albeit with somewhat different approaches. The EU AI Act represents a more stringent and comprehensive approach to AI regulation, providing rules and clear enforcement mechanisms. The Biden AI Executive Order adopts a more flexible and collaborative approach, focusing on guidance and international cooperation. Both sets of policies reflect the ongoing search for effective ways to govern AI development and deployment in a rapidly evolving landscape.

\_\_\_

Closely linked to intelligence is the question of consciousness

\_\_\_

While their approaches differ, their shared goal of responsible AI development presents an opportunity for international cooperation and learning from each other's experiences. Since AI is in a state of dynamic growth and evolution, the guardrails must be dynamic too.

The idea of superintelligence also raises several philosophical questions. Intelligence is a multifaceted concept. We associate it with the ability to learn, reason, solve problems, and adapt to new situations. But is this all there is to it? Do complex calculations and superior information processing define intelligence? Or are there other crucial elements like creativity, emotional intelligence, and social understanding?  Superintelligence would excel in processing information at unimaginable speeds and solve problems beyond human comprehension, which begs the question: would superintelligence, in its superior computational prowess, represent a true advancement over human intelligence, or simply a different form of it?

Closely linked to intelligence is the question of consciousness. We experience the world subjectively, with emotions, feelings, and a sense of self. Would superintelligence possess this quality? Some argue that consciousness is an emergent property of complex systems, and with enough processing power, superintelligence could achieve it. Others believe consciousness is unique to biological systems and cannot be replicated in machines. The answer to this question has profound implications. A conscious superintelligence might have its own desires and goals, potentially conflicting with ours. An unconscious superintelligence, while powerful, might be easier to control but raise questions about its ability to truly understand and solve human problems. Equally important is the fact that human imagination is a powerful cognitive capacity that drives creativity, problem-solving, social understanding, personal growth, and our ability to transcend the constraints of the present moment. Along with our intuition, imagination is a fundamental aspect of what makes us human and shapes our individual and collective experiences. Could superintelligence imagine, fantasize or mythologize?

Superintelligence might also prompt us to re-evaluate the very meaning of existence. If machines can surpass most of our cognitive abilities, what role is left for humanity? Perhaps superintelligence can help us explore the universe, understand consciousness, and answer timeless philosophical questions. The exploration of superintelligence is not simply a technological pursuit, but a philosophical journey. It forces us to confront fundamental questions about ourselves and our place in the universe. By grappling with these complexities, we can ensure that superintelligence, if and when it emerges, is a force for good, advancing us not just technologically, but also as a species seeking meaning and understanding.  This ongoing dialogue is crucial to navigating the legal, ethical and philosophical labyrinth posed by superintelligence. We need to understand that a superintelligent system has the right values and ethical principles aligned with human values, and becomes a collaborator enhancing our freedoms, and our humanity.
