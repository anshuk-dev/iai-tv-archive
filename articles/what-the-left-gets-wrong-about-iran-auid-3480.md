# What the Left gets wrong about Iran

*Rethinking anti-imperialist politics in the Middle East*

**By Hossein Dabbagh** | *30th January 2026* | *Read time: approx. 18 mins* | *3,645 words*

![What the Left gets wrong about Iran](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/iran-2.webp)

---

*Many on the Western Left have been silent on recent events in Iran. Some have even openly supported the Islamic Republic as a bulwark against Western imperialism, downplaying domestic coercion as the price necessary to counter Western aggression. But according to philosophers **Patrick Hassan** and **Hossein Dabbagh**, this is a fundamental mistake. Treating Iran as an anti-imperialist force fails to recognize that in foreign affairs Iran, like all other states, acts not on behalf of lofty anti-imperialist principles but according to hard-nosed realism. And even on its own terms of countering Western influence and retaining sovereignty, Iran has failed. The Left rightly denounces Western repression at home and abroad, Hassan and Dabbagh argue, and it’s time to denounce it in the case of Iran.*

The Islamic Republic of Iran is currently undergoing one of the most significant periods of domestic turmoil since its inception in 1979. At the time of writing this, credible tallies [suggest](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2026/jan/27/iran-protests-death-toll-disappeared-bodies-mass-burials-30000-dead) a vastly higher death toll than the regime admits. More than 6,000 killings have been verified, with a further 17,000 recorded deaths under investigation, implying a possible total of around 22,000. The regime has imposed a near-total digital blackout lasting over 400 hours. These protests began with strikes by the merchant class centered in the bazaars upon yet further increases to inflation in an already crippled economy, but have since ignited apparent broader dissatisfaction with the governing clerical powers among more workers’ unions and the broader population. Popular chants across Iran’s major cities have indicated this: “Death to the Dictator,” “Death to [Ayatollah] Khameini,” Iran’s current Supreme Leader, and “Until the Mullah is buried, this homeland won’t be a homeland.”

Progressive factions within the Western Left that have rightfully spoken up for the struggles of Palestinians facing Israeli aggression have, nevertheless, either been curiously [silent on recent events in Iran](https://www.persuasion.community/p/the-lefts-deafening-silence-on-iran), or have been open in their [support of the Islamic Republic](https://www.afr.com/world/middle-east/progressive-left-must-stop-making-excuses-for-iran-s-tyrants-20260108-p5nshh). Prima facie, the latter faction is especially puzzling. The Islamic Republic’s governance is characterized by autocratic rule in a theocratic framework that has overseen an increasingly stratified society with massive wealth disparity.

\_\_\_

The more fundamental philosophical issue is not partisan at all. It is a recurrent pathology of political judgements.

\_\_\_

Aside from an elite ruling class riddled with corruption scandals, the Islamic Republic has a plethora of documented human rights abuses, including but not limited to the imprisonment, torture and execution of dissident journalists, environmentalists, union leaders, religious and ethnic minorities, and individuals (usually women) who are considered to have flouted the preferred religious norms of the dominant class. Practices which are routinely (and rightly) protested against in Western liberal societies are often excused by the Left in the case of Iran, and practices that many on the Left rightly condemn when carried out by Western-aligned regimes are frequently minimized, relativized, or rationalized in the Iranian case.

That asymmetry is the puzzle this article begins from: why does a movement whose moral vocabulary is supposedly anchored in anti-domination and solidarity so often suspend those commitments when the agent of domination presents itself as “anti-imperialist”? And, crucially, what, if anything, could justify that suspension?

The more fundamental philosophical issue is not partisan at all. It is a recurrent pathology of political judgement in which opposition to one form of domination becomes so identity-forming that it licenses indifference to, or even endorsement of, domination by the other side. In geopolitics this often takes a familiar shape—camp thinking and the “enemy of my enemy” reflex—where moral evaluation tracks alignment rather than the lived realities of coercion, agency, and self-determination. Iran is the case study here, but the structure of the mistake is general.

**Understanding the** **anti-imperialist perspective**

One strategy that is sometimes taken to answer this is to outright deny that the Islamic Republic engages in such practices, and that all human rights abuses and claims of corruption or socio-economic mismanagement are subversive foreign propaganda campaigns against a country admirably standing up to Western neo-imperialism. In fact, they may say, the Islamic Republic is even highly progressive, boasting of a high literacy rate and an educated female workforce, and even forward-thinking on issues such as trans-affirming care.

This position may be found on the fringes of the internet, but a more politically serious version which seems to have [broader uptake on the Left](https://weeklyworker.co.uk/worker/1247/true-to-principle/) can be charitably reconstructed in the following way: The Islamic Republic may be domestically repressive, yet this is a regrettable but necessary price to pay for the country’s broader admirable geopolitical stance against imperialist powers (i.e., the USA, the EU, Israel) that have previously sought, and currently still seek, to undermine its sovereignty. We cannot demand the idealistic moral luxury, the argument goes, that such states be open and liberal democracies as we enjoy here, since they are pressured to take extreme measures to fight a multiplicity of subversive powers that often work behind closed doors. If the Islamic Republic falls, it holds further, the rest of the “resistance” to Western hegemony in the region falls too. In short: the enemy of my enemy is my friend.

The Western Left is not merely claiming that imperial threat exists (it does). It is often claiming something stronger and far more demanding: that the Islamic Republic’s domestic coercion is a defensible *trade-off* required by anti-imperialist realism, and that opposing the regime therefore functions, by default, as an extension of imperial strategy. The task, then, is to examine this trade-off, not by substituting liberal moralism for geopolitical analysis, but by asking whether the Islamic Republic in fact satisfies the very conditions that would be required for the “necessary evil” defense to carry any weight.

**Is IR really an “anti-imperialist” political force?**

There are two general lines of criticism to this position. The first is to challenge the claim that the Islamic Republic is in fact an “anti-imperialist” political power in the specific sense that warrants approval. The Islamic Republic’s [explicit self-conception](https://www.iranchamber.com/government/laws/constitution_ch10.php) and projected essence is one of defending the Iranian people’s sovereignty and national autonomy against (Western) imperial influence—importantly positioning themselves as the *sole* power capable of doing so—and even of exporting this ideal to other nations as part of the revolutionary project.

Yet, this narrative begins to crumble under scrutiny, because it trades on an equivocation about what “anti-imperialism” amounts to. If anti-imperialism is not merely a posture of rhetorical defiance, but a commitment to self-determination and non-domination as political goods, then it cannot be squared with a strategy that routinely treats neighboring polities as arenas for leverage rather than as communities entitled to shape their own fate.

\_\_\_

The point is not that Iran alone engages in proxy politics; many states do. The point is that proxy politics is not “anti-imperialist”.

\_\_\_

In fact, this framing ignores or misunderstands the Islamic Republic’s entrenched strategy of pragmatic “forward defense,” namely: installing and sponsoring proxy forces and militias to aid and influence the domestic affairs of countries in greater proximity to armed confrontation with perceived enemies as a means of protecting itself, rather than an open war it cannot win. The Islamic Republic has invested billions of dollars and manpower in [Syria](https://www.reuters.com/investigations/iran-had-imperial-ambitions-syria-secret-embassy-papers-show-why-it-failed-2025-05-01/), [Iraq](https://www.iiss.org/globalassets/media-library---content--migration/files/publications---free-files/strategic-dossier/iran-dossier/irans-networks-of-influence-in-the-middle-east.pdf), Lebanon, Palestine, and Yemen to the [discontent of their respective populations](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/lebanese-pm-says-he-rejects-iranian-interference-lebanese-matter-2024-10-18/), and this discontent is rooted in the very same reasons the anti-imperialist Left reasonably has disdain for—the [meddling in the autonomy of sovereign nations](https://digital-commons.usnwc.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3124&context=ils).

The point is not that Iran alone engages in proxy politics; many states do. The point is that proxy politics is not “anti-imperialist” in the morally relevant sense. It is a form of power projection that often works by weakening the autonomy of other states, embedding coercive leverage inside them, and instrumentalizing local populations for a wider strategic calculus. The Islamic Republic’s weaponizing of these foreign populations for its broader strategic aims takes the form of keeping enemy states’ greater resources bogged down in frontier conflicts, but it is thinly veiled under the moralistic rhetoric of aid for oppressed people, and specifically the broader [Islamic ummah](https://iai.tv/video/the-west-vs-the-islamic-world-with-ali-allawi). The proxies that operate on behalf of the Islamic Republic for this end have even been implicated in [serious war crimes](https://levant24.com/news/international/2024/11/icc-urged-to-investigate-iranian-backed-militias-for-war-crimes-in-syria/) within some of these countries.

Moreover, the pragmatic self-interested nature of the Islamic Republic is evidenced by its historical collaboration with these explicit enemies when it suits its aims. This was the case with the USA during the notorious [Iran-Contra Affair](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/reagan-iran/), and beyond that [even with Israel](https://iranwire.com/en/politics/135094-oil-and-weapons-irans-covert-ties-with-israel-in-the-1980s/) during the Iran-Iraq war in the 1980s, where the primary threat to the Islamic Republic’s political goals was neither Israel nor the USA, but Saddam Hussein’s Ba’athist Iraq. During this era, the Islamic Republic sent Israel millions of dollars in return for desperately needed weapons and spare parts for American-made warplanes, and even shared and received intelligence from Mossad on the Iraqi military’s movements. Their collaboration may have even resulted in their shared military goal of destroying Saddam’s under-construction Osirak (Tammuz-1) nuclear facility.

Here, the point is not the cheap accusation of “hypocrisy.” It is that the Islamic Republic behaves like a state: it treats ideology as flexible when survival is at stake, and it is willing to transact, indirectly and covertly, even with those it publicly frames as existential enemies. These collaborations show that in its proper geopolitical context, the Islamic Republic is not taking a principled stand against imperialism *simpliciter*, nor putting its resources in the service of solidarity with the oppressed, but is, like any other state, primarily interested in its own preservation, and is willing to collaborate with even its most professed enemies to do so, through whatever channels are available—precisely the kind of hard-nosed realism that the Left claims to recognize when Western governments speak the language of “values.”

**If the Islamic Republic is “anti-imperialist,” how should it be appraised?**

A second reason for suspicion over the Western Left’s sympathy for the Islamic Republic concerns the extent to which the Islamic Republic has met its own professed aims. Even if one is convinced by the claim that the Islamic Republic is genuinely motivated by an [anti-imperialist agenda](https://iai.tv/video/on-imperialism), the goals that it itself has supplied for that end, and that are repeated by the Western Left, have spectacularly failed to materialize. The central justificatory story is familiar: Iran must absorb costs, centralize power, and endure repression because it is the price of *sovereignty*, *deterrence*, and *regional resistance*. But the question is whether the regime has actually delivered the goods it claims to be buying with those costs, especially when those costs are borne by ordinary Iranians rather than by the ideological entrepreneurs who narrate them.

\_\_\_

Despite 46 years of warning against US and Israeli intervention and aggression, the Islamic Republic was completely unprepared to effectively defend the Iranian people from it.

\_\_\_

First, the Islamic Republic’s gamble in investing billions abroad for “forward defense” has not paid off. Its key allies—Assad in Syria, Hezbollah in Lebanon, Hamas in Gaza—[have been all but obliterated](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/how-irans-network-middle-east-power-faded-2025-06-12/) in a matter of months as a result of [Israeli military strikes](https://iai.tv/articles/will-israels-iranian-strategy-backfire-on-the-west-auid-2962). Geopolitically, Iran has never been more fragile. While it may now, as a result, be spending less on declawed proxy militias, ordinary Iranians are not yet seeing the material results of the cash savings, sharpening an old grievance: enormous resources expended abroad while prices soar at home and livelihoods collapse. This is epitomized in the popular Iranian protest chant, particularly among workers’ unions: “Neither Gaza, nor Lebanon, I will sacrifice my life for Iran [*nah ghazeh nah lobnan jaanam fadaye Iran*].”

Second, the Islamic Republic was itself humiliated in various open confrontations with its political enemies since October 7th 2023, including the assassination of Hamas’ political leader Ismail Haniyeh by Israel in the heart of Iran’s capital for the inauguration of Iran’s current president. This culminated in coordinated airstrikes by Israel and the USA in the 12-day war of June 2025, where enemy warplanes gained comfortable air superiority over Iran within days, destroyed air defenses and military assets, assassinated numerous Iranian military generals and nuclear scientists, and bombed Iran’s nuclear facilities—all with minimal effective response from the Islamic Republic.

Despite 46 years of warning against US and Israeli intervention and aggression, the Islamic Republic was completely unprepared to effectively defend the Iranian people from it. Not a single bomb shelter was built for Iranian citizens, nor could the Islamic Republic prevent Mossad agents operating on the ground with impunity. Despite it being the very core of the Islamic Republic’s purported mission, it systematically failed to secure Iran’s borders, protect its people, or ensure the country’s national sovereignty. This, at minimum, complicates the moral trade-off that sympathetic Western leftists implicitly endorse: *accept domestic coercion because it buys external security.* If the [security dividend](https://iai.tv/articles/iran-is-the-enemy-the-west-created-auid-3149) is, in crucial respects, not delivered, then repression begins to look less like an emergency instrument and more like a stable technology of rule, one that persists regardless of whether it achieves the goods invoked to justify it.

The significance of this point is exacerbated by how the Islamic Republic has responded to the ongoing protests of 2026. In the face of growing unrest, various Islamic Republic spokespersons, [including Khamenei himself](https://abcnews.go.com/International/iran-protests-646-killed-activists-trump-weighs-military/story?id=129156635), have alleged that though [some initial economic grievances are legitimate](https://www.ft.com/content/f9642d49-26a4-4605-888d-51ca00f6abf5), the mass protests are in fact nothing more than CIA or Mossad-inspired plots to orchestrate regime change, and for this reason ought to be suppressed. It does not help that Israeli intelligence-linked messaging has occasionally appeared to invite precisely this inference: [Mossad-linked Farsi-language posts have suggested](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-881733) they are “with” protesters “in the field.” But it is far from clear that such claims should be read literally; strategically, they are more plausibly understood as signaling and psychological warfare than as an operational confession.

Versions of this narrative have frequently been [repeated by the Western Left](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/quotable/2026/1/12/mossad-agents-are-hiding-among-iranian). One immediate problem with it, however, is that it is purposefully ambiguous about whether (a) the protestors are themselves foreign agents (or influenced by them in their action), or whether (b) among those protesting against the Islamic Regime for their own reasons are foreign agents who intend to co-opt demonstrations by inciting violence for nefarious purposes (i.e., a color revolution).

This ambiguity matters because sly equivocation between each interpretation of the claim compromises the argument. If the idea is the former, that protestors are mostly foreign agents, this is hardly plausible given the scale of protests, and disingenuously severs the current political unrest from a causally linked history of protest stretching back decades, problematically treating the last two weeks of political action in isolation. If the idea is the latter, that foreign agents are among protestors attempting to *co-opt* the movement for independent ends, it simply does not follow that the masses of Iranians in the streets have manufactured or illegitimate grievances against their government, economic or not. That would require a further, elaborate argument with a high evidence threshold. There is a long history of [CIA plants](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP90-01208R000100150161-9.pdf) and bad actors at various [civil rights protests](https://vault.fbi.gov/cointel-pro) in the US, for example, where the point was often to disrupt and fragment genuine organizing; inversely in the case of Iran, the regime invokes the mere possibility of infiltration to suggest the uprising itself is manufactured. But in neither case does the presence of bad actors, by itself, undermine the cause or erase the real grievances that brought people into the streets, as the Left would agree.

\_\_\_

Washington’s moral rhetoric is (often rightly) treated as ideological cover, while Tehran’s moral rhetoric is granted a credibility it has not earned.

\_\_\_

The real lesson to draw, it seems, would be to support political action *for the right reasons* instead of for bad reasons (such as supporting the aims of a foreign power that may or may not coincide with the people’s). However, the more significant point about this attempt to undermine the current protests is that it tacitly admits that the Islamic Republic has yet again failed to protect its own people and sovereignty. If Mossad or CIA agents *really are* operating so brazenly on the streets of every major Iranian city, this reflects incredibly poorly on a government that styles itself as the sole protector of national autonomy and the security of the citizenry. Thus, either the threat is exaggerated for justificatory theatre, or it is real, in which case the regime has failed at its core self-appointed mission. In both cases, the moral trade-off that underwrites sympathetic Western Left defenses becomes hard to sustain.

Together, these three points above give one reason to suspect that even committed anti-imperialists ought to be highly critical of the Islamic Republic’s conduct for that cause. If the reply is to be that the Islamic Republic is at least putting up a fight, and that is what counts, then the hard-nosed pragmatism at the heart of the anti-imperialist argument outlined above was in fact never pertinent, and it boils down to the same moralistic idealism criticized (often understandably) in the liberal worldview.

**Rethinking the Left’s focus**

It is beyond doubt that a host of foreign powers—both “Western” and “Eastern”—have historically had a vested interest in domestic Iranian affairs, and continue to do so. Iranians already know this better than most. In 1953, the US and UK [orchestrated a coup](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2013/aug/19/cia-admits-role-1953-iranian-coup) to oust the democratically elected Prime Minister Mohammed Mossadegh on account of his nationalist policies, specifically towards the country’s oil, which threatened their interests, choosing to reinstall the Shah of Iran, Mohammed Reza Pahlavi, who was believed at that time to be more sympathetic to Western concerns. At least a dozen current states would now relish seeing the downfall of the Islamic Republic, waiting to see how they might take advantage of regime change in the State of Nature that is the international stage. Thus, there is a degree of healthy skepticism about what the causes of political upheaval in Iran really are, and why externally imposed regime change is both [strategically and morally problematic](https://www.thephilosopher1923.org/post/when-liberation-becomes-subjugation). However, there comes a point when this healthy skepticism morphs into either fanatical dogmatism or unwarranted pessimism about the ability of Iranians to determine their own future.

Furthermore, the Left is rightfully suspicious of Western governments’ purported concern for human rights. The historical record shows such concern as highly selective and in line with other self-interested motivations. A recent and salient example is Gaza: while Western governments speak the language of civilian protection, they have continued major arms transfers and blocked ceasefire measures at the UN. No one should be so naïve as to think that President Trump’s recent [threats to intervene militarily in Iran](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/iranian-mp-warns-greater-unrest-urging-government-address-grievances-2026-01-13/)—explicitly encouraging protests while signaling “help is on its way,” alongside renewed threats of military action—is due to heartfelt compassion for the Iranian people, and not born from an eye to a controlling stake in Iran’s resources.

\_\_\_

On the contrary, it is the anti-imperialist camp that seems fervently committed to moralizing what is an abject pragmatic failure by the Islamic Republic’s own standards.

\_\_\_

But the point we wish to draw attention to is that this critical eye for underlying motivations is, curiously, entirely absent in the case of the projected identity and actions of the Iranian government (and perhaps, by extension, other states which cast the same stance). The result is an asymmetry of suspicion: Washington’s moral rhetoric is (often rightly) treated as ideological cover, while Tehran’s moral rhetoric is granted a credibility it has not earned. This is not “anti-imperialism” so much as a kind of geopolitical aesthetic: a preference for whoever speaks the language of resistance, irrespective of what they actually do with power.

This is a call for the Left to further confront its starting assumptions and put its evaluative commitments under the microscope in order for a more honest standpoint to be taken up that doesn’t fall into the same trap of naïveté. Remaining entangled in the epistemic vice of self-deception for purely partisan reasons undermines the Left’s own supposed aims—people are less likely to listen to what is perceived as mere ideological capture, and this is invited by oversimplified analyses which collapse when even lightly probed.

When the Western Left repeats the Islamic Republic’s attempts at deflection of responsibility by claiming that protests can only be wholly manufactured by foreign agents, it too hastily dismisses grass-root networks of alternative resistance that have sometimes taken years to forge under domestic repression, and makes them harder to construct in the future (hence, some Iranians feeling pushed to endorse controversial [diaspora opposition figures](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/01/14/iran-reza-pahlavi-opposition-protests/)). Moreover, it denies the Iranian people any real agency of their own, conceiving them in the same imperialist terms as mere pawns to be instrumentalized in geopolitical academic disputes divorced from material reality, and who couldn’t possibly have organic criticisms of their theocratic rulers. When a popular Iranian chant of the last decades has been “Our enemy is right here, they lie saying it's America [*doshman ma haminjast, doroogh migan Amrikast*],” perhaps that sentiment, and their voices more broadly, ought to be acknowledged as more than a mere afterthought.

We have seen that critics of the Islamic Republic’s domestic policies are sometimes dismissed by factions of the Left as pearl-clutching liberals who have an untenably idealistic expectation for moral consistency in geopolitical discourse. But note that the two arguments above rely on no such lofty ethical assumptions. On the contrary, it is the anti-imperialist camp that seems fervently committed to moralizing what is an abject pragmatic failure by the Islamic Republic’s own standards.

By means of reconciliation, what seems pertinent is indeed a shared fundamental ethical concern for the flourishing of free agency which is tethered to how we conceive of human dignity. Imperialism and liberal intervention certainly undermine this, but we have argued that so does the Leftist pessimism that the Islamic Republic, and *only* the Islamic Republic, is a requirement for ending Western hegemony in the Middle East and protecting Iranian sovereignty, and so ought to be supported. Whether the US or Israel decides to get involved in the ongoing political unrest, it will be the Iranian people who are once again instrumentalized, having their voices drowned out, and ultimately standing to lose. Only it is now not only the hawkish imperial powers or the liberal interventionists facilitating this, but it is also, paradoxically, the allegedly progressive Left.
