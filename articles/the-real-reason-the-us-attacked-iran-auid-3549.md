# The real reason the US attacked Iran

*The war against Iran is about Western hegemony, not nuclear threats*

**By Nader Hashemi** | *13th April 2026* | *Read time: approx. 13 mins* | *2,584 words*

![The real reason the US attacked Iran](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/the-real-reason-the-US-attacked-Iran-VERSION-2.webp)

*Political scientist **Nader Hashemi** here argues that Iran was attacked because it resists American power and refuses to surrender to its commands. Hashemi writes the war is about American empire, not about nuclear weapons — pointing out that Iran offered to hand over its entire stockpile of enriched uranium and was turned down — nor about protecting Iranians from one of the region's most repressive governments. It also can’t be understood exclusively as a result of Trump and Netanyahu’s personalities. What Washington wants, and has always wanted, isn't a safer Iran. It's a subservient one.*

The US-Israeli assault on Iran is, at its core, an attempt to reorder the geopolitical landscape of the Middle East. This claim challenges a common view that has sought to interpret this war as primarily a function of personalities. Specifically, Donald Trump’s authoritarian assault on the post-war international order and his close alliance with the hawkish Israeli Prime Minister, Benjamin Netanyahu. No doubt the role of both individuals has been a critical factor in explaining this drama. To understand this war exclusively via this lens, however, is to fundamentally misunderstand the relevant political context and the strategic motives that have incentivized the US and Israel to attack Iran now. At its core, this war is about solidifying Western hegemony in the Middle East. For the past 47 years, the Islamic Republic of Iran has stood in the way. With its economy in ruins, its people in revolt, and its regional alliances in disarray, Iran has never been weaker economically, politically and militarily. The time to permanently solve “the Iran problem” has arrived.

It is noteworthy that most Western governments have supported this attack to varying degrees. The initial statements from the UK’s Keir Starmer, Canada’s Mark Carney and Germany’s Friedrich Merz replicate the same arguments as the American government, albeit with more diplomatic finesse than the Truth Social postings of President Trump.

In comparing these statements, these world leaders appear to be reading from the [same political script.](https://iai.tv/articles/a-world-without-certainty-needs-its-own-grand-narratives-auid-3542) This is not an aberration. It reflects a longstanding collective Western security posture toward the Middle East. To wit, Iran is the main source of Middle East instability, it cannot be allowed to develop nuclear weapons, and the suffering people of Iran deserve liberation from their oppressors. Since these are the foundational claims invoked by Western leaders to justify the current war, they require critical scrutiny.

**Iran and Mideast stability**

The policies of the Islamic Republic of Iran have destabilized the Middle East. From Iraq to Yemen to Lebanon, to Israel/Palestine, and critically in Syria after the 2011 uprising, Iranian intervention has produced regional chaos and suffering. But can instability in the Middle East be laid exclusively at the doorstep of Iran? The parameters of the intellectual and foreign policy debate in Western capitals take this as given. What about other regional and international actors? Have they contributed to the wider instability of the Middle East.

Consider the case of Saudi Arabia. The spread of an ultraconservative form of Islam across the Muslim world has been enormously destabilizing. This has been a 50-year state-sanctioned project to buy soft power in Muslim societies, buttressed by Saudi petrodollars. Its social and political effects have been disastrous. The ideological origins of ISIS, for example, can be traced back to a Salafi-Wahhabi theological exegesis by clerics in Mecca and Medina. In Yemen, Saudi Arabia’s war against Houthis, from 2015 to 2022, and the concomitant famine led to the death of nearly 400,000 people. During this time, the UN [described the Yemen war](https://www.unrefugees.org/news/yemen-crisis-explained/) as “the world’s worst humanitarian crisis.” The defense minister who launched this war, Crown Prince Mohammed Bin Salman (MBS), now governs Saudi Arabia.

The United Arab Emirates is also a contributor to regional destabilization. The UAE acted as the key counter-revolutionary power that rolled back the democratic gains of the 2011 Arab Spring. Using its vast wealth, arms supply, and its media and diplomatic connections, Abu Dhabi played a major role in blocking democratization in the Arab world, particularly in Egypt. Emirati officials worked closely with Egyptian intelligence and military officials to stage a coup in 2013 that ended Egypt’s democratic experiment. It then invested heavily in propping up General El-Sisi as the new dictator. Today, El-Sisi presides over a police state while the masses sink further into poverty. The UAE has also directly intervened militarily in Bahrain, Libya and, most critically, in Sudan, where the UAE-backed Rapid Support Forces (RSF) are conducting a genocide.

Israel’s genocide in Gaza is well-documented. According to [Amnesty International](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/11/israels-genocide-against-palestinians-in-gaza-continues-unabated-despite-ceasefire/), despite the US-backed ceasefire of October 2025, the genocide is ongoing, albeit at a lower level. A key consequence is that Gaza has rocked the Arab-Islamic world in numerous ways, specifically by expanding the crisis of legitimacy facing ruling regimes. Many Arabs and Muslims blame their leaders for their spineless abandonment of the Palestinians. The Arab world’s lack of intervention to end the genocide has significantly exacerbated the crisis of state-society relations. The long-term effects of Israel’s assault on Gaza will reveal themselves slowly. In the context of regional destabilization, Israel subsequently invoked its national security to launch military strikes on six countries in the Middle East (Lebanon, Syria, Yemen, Iran, Iraq, and Qatar).

Finally, we must consider the actions of the United States. By a wide margin, the most destabilizing event from the past 25 years has been the American invasion and occupation of Iraq. The full economic cost and human death toll of this transformative event are still being calculated. According to Brown University’s [Cost of War project](https://costsofwar.watson.brown.edu/papers/how-death-outlives-war), the American “war on terror” produced “an estimated 3.6-3.8 million indirect deaths in post-9/11 war zones, including Afghanistan, Pakistan, Iraq, Syria, and Yemen. The total death toll in these war zones could be at least 4.5-4.7 million and counting.”

**Nuclear weapons**

The main goal of the US-Israeli attack on Iran, as Trump has repeatedly [stated](https://www.whitehouse.gov/articles/2026/03/peace-through-strength-president-trump-launches-operation-epic-fury-to-crush-iranian-regime-end-nuclear-threat/), is “to eliminate the imminent nuclear threat posed by the Iranian regime.” Every Western leader has invoked the same concern. It is the foundational justification for the US-Israeli war against Iran.

Much of the debate on Iran’s nuclear program ignores two relevant facts. First, Iran’s nuclear threat was eliminated in 2015 when then-President Obama reached a landmark nuclear deal with Iran known as the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA). Its preamble states that “Iran reaffirms that under no circumstances will Iran ever seek, develop or acquire any nuclear weapons.” The JCPOA was backed by a UN Security Council resolution. Apart from war hawks in the US and Israel, most of the world celebrated this moment as a major contribution to international peace and security. In 2018, at the encouragement of Netanyahu, Trump revoked the JCPOA, despite Iran’s transparent implementation of the agreement. One can draw a straight line between this decision and the current war in the Middle East.

Moreover, Iran subsequently agreed to a major concession on its nuclear program. In the last round of US-Iran negotiations in Geneva on February 26 of this year, Iran offered to turn over its entire stockpile of enriched uranium, effectively precluding it from ever building a bomb. The Omani foreign minister was the principal mediator of these discussions. He [described the Iranian concession](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/full-transcript-omani-foreign-minister-badr-albusaidi/) as a “very important breakthrough that has never been achieved any time before.” The Americans rejected the offer. Trump had a larger goal in mind, a Venezuela-style [capitulation to American diktats](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/03/02/us/politics/trump-war-iran-israel.html). When Iran refused to surrender to those terms, Trump gave the order to attack.

**The people of Iran**

Self-determination for the people of Iran is the third justification for this war. Western leaders have jointly invoked this point. Iranians live under a relentlessly repressive regime, arguably the most oppressive in the Middle East. In early January, for example, a massacre of thousands of street protesters took place in Iran. Trump’s on-and-off-again call for regime change is frequently interpreted as a function of his support for the democratic rights of Iranians.

This claim is dubious at best. During the US-Iran negotiations in February 2026, for example, a discussion of human rights was not on the agenda. Had they been, Trump could have demanded the release of the more than [50,000](https://www.en-hrana.org/the-crimson-winter-a-50-day-record-of-irans-2025-2026-nationwide-protests/) political prisoners who were arrested after the massacre in January. European leaders also could have insisted that Tehran permit full access to the UN Fact-Finding Mission into Iran to investigate the bloody crackdown.

No Western government made these demands or anything close to them. Western leaders invoke the language of human rights and democracy in relation to Iran, yet their commitment rarely extends beyond rhetoric. Over the past twenty-five years of EU-US versus Iran negotiations, the focus has been exclusively on a single issue: nuclear weapons. The West’s support for authoritarian rulers across the Middle East necessarily prohibits meaningful support for human rights and democracy across the region. Emancipatory ideals are invoked by Western governments because they shroud criticism of Iran in a mantle of moral righteousness. The West’s real cause of concern when it comes to Iran is its policies that challenge Western interests in the region.

If the [repressive structure of power](https://iai.tv/articles/iran-is-the-enemy-the-west-created-auid-3149) in Iran were to remain unchanged but its leaders abandoned the nuclear program, forswore regional alliances and recognized the state of Israel in the context of the Abraham Accords, it is likely that criticism of Iranian state repression would evaporate. Evidence of this is easily discernible. When was the last time you heard the leaders of France, the UK, Canada, or Germany raise the issue of human rights abuses in the deeply repressive states of Egypt, Saudi Arabia, or Bahrain? In short, the core challenge that Iran poses for the West is not internal state repression; it is external resistance to Western policies.

**The real reason for the war in Iran**

Iran operates outside of the geo-strategic orbit of Western security concerns in the Middle East. This contrasts sharply with the Iranian monarchy that was toppled in the 1979 Iranian Revolution. The regime of the deposed Shah of Iran was a close American ally, similar to the many family dictatorships that dominate the political landscape of the Middle East today. In this important sense, the Islamic Republic is an independent actor in the region. This does not necessarily mean it is a good actor in terms of regional or internal policies. Far from it. It simply means that by virtue of its independence and its attempt to challenge Western interests in the region, Iran has become a pariah state. Recall, Iran’s nuclear program began under the Shah of Iran with American support. Had the 1979 Revolution not taken place, it is entirely possible that a nuclear-armed Iran sympathetic to Western interests would have emerged and been acceptable.

Iran is a threat to the national security interests of the United States in an analogous way to that of Cuba. It resists the American empire and refuses to surrender to its diktats. During the Cold War, instability in Latin America was primarily blamed on Fidel Castro’s policies. The parallels with Iran come into view. The Cuban Revolution, like the Iranian Revolution, became a political beacon for revolutionary forces that sought to challenge an American-centered regional order. Like Iran, Cuba is much weaker today. Nonetheless, it has been a longstanding irritant and an obsession in the political imagination of American war hawks. It is not a coincidence that at the precise moment the US is seeking regime change in Iran, it is attempting to facilitate the same outcome in Havana. Both countries are substantially weaker economically, militarily and politically. “Cuba is going to fall pretty soon,” [Trump has stated](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/06/politics/trump-cuba-marco-rubio-fall). The core geo-strategic objectives in Latin America and the Middle East are identical: expanding American hegemony while breaking the back of longstanding adversaries.

Senior American officials have explicitly confirmed this broad argument. “Operation Epic Fury,” the Under Secretary of Defense for Policy, [Elbridge Colby has announced](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/_VvAO8Akz_4), is focused on “degrading and destroying the Islamic Republic of Iran’s ability to project military power in the region.” While regime change would be ideal, turning Iran into another weak state that cannot project power beyond its borders, like Libya, Lebanon or Syria is the desired goal. Louisiana Senator John Kennedy [has been more forthright](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BV9BrCWZaBM). “The goal here is for the leadership in Iran to be unable to defend itself.” These views have long been part of the mainstream US political debate on Iran, especially among Republicans. Previous American presidents were not willing to go this far.

In 2007, Senator John McCain responded to a question from a reporter by [jokingly singing](https://www.npr.org/2007/04/20/9688222/jesting-mccain-sings-bomb-bomb-bomb-iran) “bomb, bomb, bomb, bomb, bomb Iran” to the tune of “Barbara Ann” by the Beach Boys. Senator Lindsey Graham, one of the strongest defenders of the current war, supported bombing Iran sixteen years ago. In 2010, he [told an audience at a security conference](https://www.cnn.com/2010/POLITICS/11/06/iran.us.graham/index.html) that “I think we’re to the point now that you have to really neuter the regime’s ability to wage war against us and our allies… It’s not a ground invasion but it certainly destroys the ability of the regime to strike back.”

**Conclusion**

It is important not to misread the US-Israel war on Iran. To interpret this war exclusively as a function of the unique idiosyncrasies of President Trump or [the critical role that Prime Minister Netanyahu has played](https://zeteo.com/p/netanyahu-trump-iran-war-israel) is to misunderstand the deeper structural reasons that repeatedly draw the West back into the Middle East.

[According to NATO's Secretary-General](https://x.com/thorstenbenner/status/2029993559743463702), among key European leaders “there is widespread support for what the president is doing.” This reflects a longstanding consensus among Western governments about the sources of stability in the Middle East and those allegedly responsible for any instability. Israel and Arab autocrats fall into the former category, Iran and its allies in the latter.

Formulating an effective response to Iran’s policies has been the subject of intense debate over the years. Critically, this debate has been about tactics, not goals. The pre-eminent objective shared by all Western states is that the Middle East should remain under Western influence. Any state or non-state actor that challenges Western hegemony [must be confronted](https://iai.tv/articles/we-must-fight-to-prevent-war-auid-3443). For 47 years, Iran’s uncompromising defiance of a Western and increasingly Israeli-centric regional order has been at the root of this crisis. Trump’s former EU Ambassador Gordon Sondland [could not have been more explicit](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/04/world/video/european-response-israeli-us-iran-strikes). “We are taking risks with Israel to once and for all fix this problem.”
