# We need a new political theology

*Rediscovering a political theology*

**By Geminello Pretrossi** | *18th April 2025* | *Read time: approx. 16 mins* | *3,255 words*

![We need a new political theology](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/liberal-philosophy-has-lost-the-ideological-battle.webp)

---

[*Italian legal and political philosopher Geminello Preterossi*](https://docenti.unisa.it/004697/en/curriculum) *argues that, as Trump and others’ victory around the world shows us, the liberal political project has failed. He ascribes this to liberals’ own blindness to the effects of their politics on the world around them. His suggestion to those who want to overturn Trump and co. is twofold: firstly, we must engage in a rigorous and merciless self-critique, which he begins in this piece; and secondly, we must rediscover a political theology that can unite large groups of people around a new political project, starting from the small examples of sacredness we see in our world now.*

We live in a special time: while history is kicking back into gear thanks to the great geopolitical transformations of our moment, Western elites seem to live in a closed-off aquarium of oft-repeated clichés considered to be liberal and “progressive”. The effect is a break from reality: we, or Western liberals, have entered a psycho-political plane.

\_\_\_

Over the course of the last decades, and with the decisive victory of neoliberalism, we as a society, and I am broadly speaking of the West, have lost the plot. We no longer have the theologies – religious, political, or otherwise – that tied us together.

\_\_\_

This can only produce disasters and inhibit any critical or political antidotes. Yet it now seems impossible to subtract ourselves from the reigning logic. That would reveal the cracks in our reality that pretends to be absolute. This is key: the liberal worldview presents itself as absolutist, pretending it has the ability to know and incorporate everything within itself. Neoliberalism, which is anti-dialectical (i.e., it refuses a back-and-forth exchange with other phenomena or realities that could lead to synthesis), denies the process which has been the bedrock of Western societies and which led to its own existence: the transcendence of individual interests and experiences into the collective social forms of political, judicial, and secular institutions. That, in a nutshell, would be politics [and political theology](https://platypus1917.org/wp-content/uploads/Political-Theology_-Four-Chapte-Carl-Schmitt.pdf). Yet neoliberalism’s very denial of this political process is in itself political.

Over the course of the last decades, and with the decisive victory of neoliberalism, we as a society, and I am broadly speaking of the West, have lost the plot. We no longer have the theologies – religious, political, or otherwise – that tied us together and helped us find (imperfect) cohesion and transcendence directed towards achieving some political horizon. We need a new political theology: a form of shared meaning that can transcend individual interests and unite us in a shared purpose.  This political theology, I believe, should take up the tradition of the last century and ground itself in collective political meaning-making projects, even at a smaller scale, such as at the neighborhood level. But first, I believe, we must engage in a rigorous and critical act of self-analysis of our recent trajectory. We must call the issues by name. Part of the reason our current political paradigm is so successful is that it refutes such reckoning. This article, on the contrary, attempts to recover it.

**The death of God**

When (religious) faith disappeared around the onset of Modernity, great ideologies took its place. Now we find ourselves in a moment when those visions of the world have also dissipated, and all grand visions are suppressed besides that which denies all others (i.e., neoliberalism), defending only an eternal economics-ruled presentism. This vacuum of visions is inevitably filled by something else. For instance, [the pseudo-spirituality of new age beliefs](http://iai.tv/video/the-shadow-of-spirituality), newfound scientific positivism, [the worship of technology](http://iai.tv/video/babette-babich-nietzsche-the-birth-of-tragedy-and-the-technology-trap), [patriarchal right-wing religious revivals](http://iai.tv/video/the-rise-of-the-new-right): post-human and post-political trends of various kinds.

Today’s Western societies are the first in history to live without gods. In early and late Modernity (17th-19th centuries), these were replaced with surrogates such as the State and political parties, which worked well enough despite being lesser secularized versions of religious authority. Now we live in a time outside of time, consisting in semblances, in bogus divinities: markets that we claim can “vote”, freedom equated with auto-entrepreneurialism, unfettered competition, asymmetrical and discriminatory humanitarianism. I am talking about fictitious and mediocre “absolutes”. They are no longer the object of bottom-up fervor, as religion or certain ideologies were, but rather represent top-down imposed dogmas, increasingly unstable. The increase in censorship of the last decades and the demonization of dissent are also signs of this extreme fragility, revealing the hypocrisy of liberal-democratic “values.” In sum, neoliberalism turned nasty after the 2008 financial crisis because it doesn’t work; but the supposed recipes to fix it are failing also.

[It should not surprise us that our current Western liberal “absolutes” have revealed themselves to be such imperfect substitutes to the previous theologies](http://iai.tv/articles/the-return-of-meaning-auid-2043), being both anti-religion and anti-state, and thus denying us any possible transcendence. Some of their features include the absence of a dialectical relationship between the secular and the sacred; the rejection of faith in politics; the repulsion towards the people in the flesh and blood; the debasement of identities (perceived only as accumulations of artificial and ahistorical details); the stigmatization of belonging in any form. We are erasing history, even our own.

In short, without any gods we are engaged in a rage against ourselves, heralding more than a “sunset” (the West has always been sunsetting), but an auto-destructive catastrophe. We have entered a kind of [“neo-fundamentalism”, both nihilistic and moralistic](http://iai.tv/articles/the-metaphysics-behind-putins-war-on-liberalism-auid-3120), without a spirit or guiding idea, just hysteria and irrationality. This explains the blindness of today’s “progressive” elites, made abundantly clear by Trump’s second victory. The election results came [once again as a shock](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2024/nov/07/stephen-colbert-trump-election-win) and their causes have not yet been sufficiently analyzed or understood, as liberals concentrate instead on searching for alibis and [making use of pre-cooked ideological frameworks](https://vcresearch.berkeley.edu/news/economy-sexism-and-conspiracies-fueled-trumps-reelection). Liberal common sense has entered a dead end.

There are multiple causes of this decadence, but in general they are conducible to the bubbles created, especially in the West, by globalization and neoliberalism. The shift has been anthropological: the sense of tragedy in-built in the human condition has been banned. It must be so. Either because history has ended (the ideological triviality with which [Fukuyama voiced the common feeling of Western elites post-1989](http://iai.tv/articles/francis-fukuyama-warns-trump-is-not-a-realist-auid-3128)), or because we should not think of certain things due to “political correctness.”

The final result is the inability to understand reality: deception and auto-deception. It should not be surprising that all those who do not live in our privileged Western bubbles – the fragmented and abandoned masses, those in the global peripheries – are reacting with disgust at that which is still termed “progress.” This opens up avenues to the Right, which appears more at ease with reality, at least rhetorically – though it is also, as the Left, tied to the absolutism of the global hierarchy (the EU, NATO, UN), and concerned with a mere management of existence, rather than politics.

**Taking stock**

The only way we can start rectifying our predicament is by being very honest with ourselves. So let’s look our situation in the face: nothing is left of class conflict besides a convenient farce to reference every now and then, instrumentally, without ever really calling into question the neoliberalization of the State. Ongoing neoliberalization is what is actually undermining democracy by [sacrificing social inclusion and political participation to finance and technology](http://iai.tv/video/the-new-ruling-class-yanis-varoufakis).

Labor disappears. Capitalism, a complex structure always in transformation, is moved to the background. At the forefront are we, individuals, who “never do enough.” Science and technology are “good in themselves” – it is us who must redeem ourselves and be good in using them. Globalization is also “good in itself,” as long as we seek a “different,” good globalization, then all of the world’s problems will be solved, conflicts will disappear. Humanity returns to “nature” and exits from history: pacified, peaceful, and post-political. Moralism prevails on criticism and political action.

The body is the only thing that counts. Its potential and its artificial survival over time are the only possible horizon of sense for us, the only human hope. Any evocation of tradition, spirituality, the sacred, or even secularization is suspect. Everything can be bought and sold: this is freedom. Surrogacy, for instance, does not pose any problem, it does not commodify women’s bodies. [State-sanctioned euthanasia](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-us-canada-64004329), even for adolescents and the depressed, becomes admissible. Abortion is no longer a necessary drama, but a “fundamental right.” In general, the tragic dimension of life is denied. The horizon of neoliberalism and globalization is techno-economic nihilism. But without the grand Abyss, the Nietzschean trembling. A sunset, but on the most absolute mediocrity.

Neoliberalism is like a cancer that spreads everywhere and branches out opportunistically, exploiting the life of the host organism until it degenerates the surrounding tissues. It would pretend to have brought humanity to its final stage, a pacified and eternal totalizing order guaranteed by the market, free of imbalances, asymmetries, injustices, conflicts. This is obviously not the case. But [the blindness of Western elites continues](http://iai.tv/video/mark-littlewood-the-case-for-classical-liberalism) and actually becomes more furious in the face of the increasing difficulties of maintaining a semblance of order, and of the evident inefficacy of the pseudo-solutions they propose *ad infinitum*. They refuse to admit their epochal failure, which would help initiate an undoing of finance-capitalism. The mask has fallen as social alienation and atomization unfold before our eyes in a series of crises and emergencies that have become the “new normal.” But the weight of financial interests, as well as the [classic defensive strategy of “negation”](https://journal-psychoanalysis.eu/articles/the-birth-of-thinking-from-the-spirit-of-negation/#:~:text=Negation%20is%20a%20way%20of%20taking%20cognizance%20of,from%20the%20affective%20process.%20%28Freud%2C%201925%2C%20p.%20233-240%29) – the Freudian psychological defense mechanism in which we recognize something intellectually while emotionally rejecting it –  prevent us from genuinely acknowledging reality.

We must have the courage to recognize, and make explicit, each of the points of failure of the Left on the timeline towards neoliberalism. The drama of the Left was not that it did not have sufficient ideological resources, but that those it did have were mostly ambivalent (e.g., in the 1968 movement, pitching an individualistic against a collective focus). Out of such ambiguity was born an easy pragmatism (the precursor to opportunism) that has facilitated the current shift to the Right.

\_\_\_

We seem to have lost the ability to look at things for what they are, to critically assess, rather than remove, whatever may be challenging and degrading, but which is nonetheless necessary to comprehend.

\_\_\_

Meanwhile the Left’s legitimate intellectual baggage ([for instance, Gramsci’s *Prison Notebooks*](https://uberty.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/gramsci-prison-notebooks.pdf)) was liquidated. Recovering and restarting this tradition now is a titanic enterprise. Necessary, of course. But possible, in the West at least, only if we begin from rigorous and honest soul-searching. If we really want to identify the deep reasons for a historical defeat and the political status quo that followed, and not limit ourselves to treating this shift as a natural event without specific causes or responsibilities, we must be conceptually trenchant, critically merciless even. Otherwise we absolve ourselves without understanding, and carry on in our mistakes. We must avoid the obsession with political events, superficial contingencies, but rather focus on the deeper issues, adopting a meta- (or pre-) political approach: one that is symbolic, anthropological, critically realist. Only this way can we recover the sense and substance of politics.

We must not be afraid to ask ourselves: how is it possible that the very foundations of 20th century political culture have been destroyed? We seem to have lost the ability to look at things for what they are, to critically assess, rather than remove, whatever may be challenging and degrading, but which is nonetheless necessary to comprehend. This attitude does not describe a flat and accommodating realism, but rather the premise for reading reality and elaborating effective, even transformative, political actions in response. Conflict, power, strategy: these are essential notions in reality and in politics. But today’s neoliberal culture and progressive common sense are dominated by a wholly different narration, one that is both moralizing and polemical, both consolatory and cynical, absolutist and arrogant. From Machiavelli, Hegel, and Gramsci to the mediocrity of today’s moralistic left-wing culture.

Until we understand that power exists, that conflict is necessary, that the weight of interests continues, that ideologies exist even when they hide themselves, that the world is plural (and thus not made in our image), we will never recover a serious and critical political analysis. And so never open up a space of concrete possibility to generate that extra transcendence – or political theology – that we desperately need, as it is the premise for having real politics and not its simulacrum. Real politics as in a politics of substance and transformation, rather than of good administration.

**What next: Re-creating transcendence**

What role can the realms of art, religion, ideology, and critical thought play today? Our personal preferences, psychological anxieties, dietary choices, sensibilities towards animals or nature – these are all subjective experiences. But motivating them is also a deeply spiritual, and even political, instinct, which desires more than a purely administered world. I believe it is right to try to politicize these fields. That does not mean turning them into a daily polarizing struggle, but the opposite. I think it means taking a step back rather than forward, and discussing these issues openly, questioning our very assumptions.

Certain small devotional acts, [such as the increase in spiritual practices like meditation, point to the reemergence of new questions around meaning](http://iai.tv/video/solving-the-meaning-crisis-john-vervaeke) that are irreducible to do-it-yourself or New Age religions, but instead contain a political nucleus, even if neglected. Might the use of colorful reusable water bottles instead of plastic ones not be a tiny act of secularized devotion? A sense of mystery and sacredness is also relevant for secular politics. Until we understand this fact completely we will never be able to understand the heart of modern politics, it will always slip away. And talking about the importance of the sacred does not mean assuming a religious perspective.

But we must always ask ourselves if what we do really represents an ethical and political movement, something to belong to, or if it is only a false wave, even if in good faith, easily co-optable by neoliberal ideological apparatuses. If nothing else, with Trump, liberal pseudo-progressivism has been called into question, and that is a good thing. But this is not happening in the name of overcoming techno-capitalism and financial domination, but rather to reconfigure society for the sake of national US territories, production, and social groups. *America first*.

The sacralization of the *homo economicus* (the economic human) under neoliberalism on the one hand, and the reemergence of polemic and identitarian strains of religious belonging on the other, seem to suggest the unexpected impossibility to extinguish not merely the theological, but some ethical-religious dimension within humanity. The question of religious meaning and belonging must not be denigrated. Do-it-yourself and New Age religions represent a private, rather than collective, transcendence, adapted to the West’s fully secularized and hyper-individualistic paradigm. But the existential agitation they represent reveals the need for *something more*. If we want to prove Nietzsche wrong – [avoid his prediction that after the death of God](https://philoslugs.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/the-gay-science-friedrich-nietzsche.pdf), the same forces will bring about the death of the “mortal god” of the State, and that privatization will then dominate – we must ensure that the secularization of the modern State and law do not remain confined within the bureaucratic-procedural machine, absorbed by economic forces. To secularize, but not *fully*, we need forms of collective transcendence. And vice-versa: in order for forms of collective transcendence to develop, we cannot bring the process of secularization to its nihilistic extreme.

\_\_\_

Today there’s a desert. And in this social desert generated by the absolutism of the market, we have a hard time finding even that drop of meaning that helps us not find ourselves alone before an abyss.

\_\_\_

I do not want to be misunderstood: it is not at all necessary that that missing piece (our sense of needing “something more”), take on a religious shape. It can also be ethical-political or ideological. But the point is that this ethical substance cannot be resolved in mere individual compensation, and avoid a collective identity. Ethics is not rhetoric. At the same time, in our age, our so-called “values” can no longer be considered objective: unquestionable and immutable. Whatever they will be, they will necessarily contain a shared dimension with community-oriented implications, while also being pluralistic and intersubjective. Importantly, this former shared character does not necessarily contain a repressive, traditional ethos. Additionally, it must be said that a collective transcendence cannot be *imposed* over a people as an oppressive totality; it must be co-generated and recognized by those people.

Of course, in many ways neoliberalism also presents itself as a transcendent religion today (or a vision of the world), and asks dogmatic adhesion of its followers. But neoliberalism achieves this by dissembling itself. [Proclaiming the end of all political faith, it denies its own theological-political nature, and asserts the impossibility of being transcended](https://www.thevenicereview.com/p/political-theology-in-the-21st-century). Yet even this particular neoliberal individualism absorbs us, despite seeming incapable of answering our existential questioning. The drying up of our ability to imagine different lives is also a result of the schism between subject and community, which, sanctifying the individual against the collective, ends up making them both infertile. Today there’s a desert. And in this social desert generated by the absolutism of the market, we have a hard time finding even that drop of meaning that helps us not find ourselves alone before an abyss.

\_\_\_

We need the reanimation of sites of collective transcendence opposed to the reigning paradigm, building on existing attempts at finding the sacred.

\_\_\_

The 20th century failure of secular substitutes for religion, especially of communism, had a traumatic effect on progress, nullifying for a long while any alternatives to the reigning hegemony. The problem, therefore, is not so much secularization as the disenchantment provoked by the fall of its idols. The point now is to understand whether such a fall inhibits the possibility of a new intense political imaginary. [For instance, one answering to environmental themes in a structural way](http://iai.tv/articles/liberal-democracies-cannot-solve-climate-change-auid-2108), tied in with a critique of capitalism that avoids moralism and the reign of purely subjective testimony. On the one hand, we need a broader horizon; on the other, our going beyond cannot aspire for a Garden of Eden that overcomes conflict, ambivalences, the reality of power. After all, even if a unified and peaceful humanity were conceivable, we should ask ourselves if it would be desirable, if it would not lose something of its vitality and creativity, if such a uniformizing process would not result in a homogenization of cultural difference and the hierarchization of populations.

It is necessary to unite great ideals with a realistic look on the human condition and its political possibilities. The fights for principles and needs can only achieve concrete results if they look at the actual conditions of reality and if they face the issue of power. The fact that reality can never be perfectly appeased is a resource that immunizes us against stagnation and self-deception. In conclusion, I believe the only way out of our political strait is through, firstly, the clear-eyed acceptance of where we are, why, and what this means. We need to understand neoliberalism’s destruction of social bonds. We need to recognize our shared need for transcendence of the individual self that individualism can never satisfy. We need to confront the political void that market absolutism has left in its wake. And, secondly, we need the reanimation of sites of collective transcendence opposed to the reigning paradigm, building on existing attempts at finding the sacred. So, yes, sites of worship and political parties; but also perhaps other forms, such as community hubs and environmentalist meet-ups. We need to concretize our need for “something more” into a livable and shareable experience.
