# To unify relativity and quantum mechanics we must abandon materialism

*There is something more fundamental than both mind and matter*

**By Dean Rickles** | *20th June 2025* | *Read time: approx. 11 mins* | *2,163 words*

![To unify relativity and quantum mechanics we must abandon materialism](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/To-solve-quantum-gravity-we-must-go-beyond-the-physical.webp)

---

*Physicists have so far failed to unify general relativity and quantum mechanics. As attempts to unite them into a quantum theory of gravity mount up, philosopher of physics Dean Rickles argues that the assumption of materialism is the problem. We need to look beyond the physical—beyond space, time and matter—to something primordial out of which minds can construct physical reality, and which explains both general relativity and quantum mechanics. Pioneers like John Wheeler and David Bohm have already begun to chart what such a realm of “pre-physics” might look like—it’s high time physics took their ideas more seriously.*

A pair of recent physics Nobel prizes ([2020](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2020/summary/) and [2022](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2022/summary/)) were awarded for basic research in general relativity (Einstein’s theory of gravitation that explains gravity as the curvature of spacetime by matter and energy) and quantum mechanics (our best bet for a theory *of matter and energy*). The experimental successes of these theories keep piling up. There is clearly much truth in them. They both aim to describe the same world: *this* world. They should surely overlap, since the matter and energy described by quantum mechanics should curve spacetime as well as good old-fashioned non-quantum mechanical matter and energy. Why then can we not construct a theory in which they both appear? Why is it [so difficult to build what would be a Quantum Theory of Gravity](http://iai.tv/articles/the-crisis-of-quantum-gravity-auid-1769)?

\_\_\_

It is the biggest scandal in physics, but it is rarely presented as anything other than an amusing paradox or an interesting challenge.

\_\_\_

It seems, in crucial ways, their *pictures* of the world are completely at odds. Quantum theory speaks of particles and fields in a *fixed* spacetime that stands quite apart from them. General relativity speaks of a spacetime that gregariously joins in the drama with particles and fields. Demanding the particles and fields obey quantum mechanics spoils their passionate tango with gravity and spacetime. Making the spacetime move and curve by the matter and energy described by quantum theories, spoils the tight rules of quantum theory that make it go. We are as a result in [a more-than-century-old stalemate](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/covered-with-deep-mist-9780199602957?cc=au&lang=en&). And consider what this means for our scientific worldview: if these two theoretical frameworks cannot peacefully co-exist, then there is no solid ground for physics to stand on at a fundamental level. It is the biggest scandal in physics, but it is rarely presented as anything other than an amusing paradox or an interesting challenge. It’s a mess!

\_\_\_

The shackles of orthodox, materialist approaches to doing physics must at the very least be loosened, if not broken off completely to make progress.

\_\_\_

There have been bold and beautiful attempts to clear up the mess. Some approaches such as [string theory](http://iai.tv/video/the-trouble-with-string-theory) (which is, in fact, far more than just a quantum theory of gravity) and [loop quantum gravity](http://iai.tv/articles/quantum-mechanics-white-holes-and-the-relational-world-auid-3085) have even made their way into pop culture. But they still face what appear to be problems with generating specific predictions or else serious consistency problems when trying to cast the ideas as kosher physical theories. After battling through a multi-decade investigation of the history and philosophy of this stubbornly persistent problem, I believe it is time to face the music and to accept that something has gone terribly wrong. It is not lack of effort or skill. The brilliance of the minds that have battled with this stubbornly persistent problem cannot be underestimated. Pretty much every approach has been pursued and exhausted. I strongly suspect that the shackles of [orthodox, materialist approaches](http://iai.tv/video/truth-theory-and-ultimate-reality) to doing physics must at the very least be loosened, if not broken off completely to make progress. I will briefly explain why and, even more briefly, what shape this might take.

\_\_\_

The approach I suggest we adopt is to deny that there is any kind of constitutive or direct causal link between gravity and quantum mechanics. Instead, they have the same source.

\_\_\_

Firstly, perhaps we could consider another less radical direction, namely to [sever or ignore the link between our models of physics and reality](http://iai.tv/articles/reality-is-not-revealed-by-quantum-mechanics-auid-2512), and focus on experimental outputs and observations only—treating the theoretical models behind them as [useful fairytales](http://iai.tv/articles/cosmologys-crisis-challenges-scientific-realism-david-merritt-auid-2651) that simply help us systematize our experience? This “forget truth” idea infiltrated post-WWII physics with the “[shut up and calculate](http://iai.tv/articles/einstein-vs-bohr-quantum-reality-is-still-up-for-grabs-auid-3131)” mantra, coined by David Mermin. In this atmosphere, which persists in some circles, it is a source of embarrassment to discuss what the theory means and ask whether it is true, especially as regards quantum theory which was Mermin’s target (though he himself was against the idea). But while this approach can be useful in some circumstances, to make specific types of progress, it can have the side-effect of blocking off new paths for exploration—including the kinds of thinking that led to the very theories that physicists were shutting up and calculating with, which could never have emerged from such a blinkered methodology.

What is needed, rather, is the same kind of deep, open-minded thinking that led to quantum theory and general relativity, if we are to move beyond them. But we must still account for the aforementioned successes of these theories. They must be present and accounted for in some sense. Is there some framework that can help? The approach I suggest we adopt is to deny that there is any kind of constitutive or direct causal link between gravity and quantum mechanics. Instead, they have the same source. Neither is more fundamental: they both result from a deeper structure, as yet unexplored. I think something of this sort, in which both quantum matter and gravity are built from something that exhibits neither, offers the best bet for breaking the stalemate.

Indeed, we might take the currently fashionable dualities between quantum mechanics and gravity as evidence for just such a deeper layer. Such dualities (e.g. that simply expressed as “[GR=QM” by Leonard Susskind](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1708.03040)) establish a kind of theoretical dictionary between the elements of the two theories, so that one can transform between them, doing calculations in one to figure out information about the other, and vice versa. Neither is considered more fundamental than the other, and one cannot be reduced to the other. But given their completely different world-pictures, we are pushed into viewing neither picture as fundamental and looking instead for the common ground of which they are both realizations, albeit in different clothing. However, since we are mixing up spacetime descriptions and quantum descriptions in such dualities, it suggests that neither is ultimate, and we likely must look elsewhere—beyond spacetime, and beyond quantum matter—for our building materials.

We face a constructive task. As John Archibald Wheeler liked to put it: “How Build?” There were and are pioneering souls who have tried to probe outside the limitations of our current worldview and explore this new virgin territory, [looking for something deeper than space, time, and matter, that can act as a kind of primordial building material](http://iai.tv/video/the-reality-beyond-spacetime) out of which both general relativity and quantum theory would be constructed in a unified manner. Wheeler himself offered several suggestions which he subsumed under the banner of “pre-geometry,” which would provide a foundation unlike anything else in physics.

\_\_\_

A radical inversion of the Copernican revolution was required: observers, including humans, needed to be moved back to the center of physics, as the basis on which physical.

\_\_\_

His initial thought was to extend an old idea (including Einstein’s later vision) of trying to build everything, including quantum systems, from spacetime alone. But it proved impossible to cover all the phenomena of quantum theory, and the discoveries of gravitational collapse—including black holes and the idea of a final “big crunch” in which all of space, time, matter, and laws end—ultimately led Wheeler to the view that our entire inventory of physics (space, time, matter, laws, and “constants” of Nature) [cannot be viewed as building blocks](https://www.quantamagazine.org/john-wheeler-saw-the-tear-in-reality-20240925/).

His mature view was that a radical inversion of the Copernican revolution was required: observers, including humans, needed to be moved back to the center of physics, as the basis on which physical reality is constructed. According to Wheeler, what he called “observer-participators” (such as humans) ask questions of nature that deliver a “Yes” or a “No” (or a “1” or a “0”), which become the bits in his now infamous expression [“It from Bit.”](https://philarchive.org/rec/WHEIPQ) On this view, the world, understood as a spacetime with material particles and fields, has no meaning (is only a potentiality) until a specific question is asked (resulting in an observation or experiment to allow the world to respond with a definite record or phenomenon). An open problem is elucidating just what counts as an observer, though it seems that the ability to create meaningful information is at the core. This is clearly a far cry from a materialist foundation: it is, and Wheeler uses the term himself, “immaterial.” A loop-like process bootstraps the world into existence from what is ultimately ineffable, [the nothingness of the old sages](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/23115/). Spacetime, matter and energy don’t just sit out there, existing by themselves: they come into being because of acts of observation. Wheeler therefore called this the “participatory universe”: we don’t just live in the universe—we help bring it into being by interacting with it. We each invoke our own kind of freedom: our freedom in what we ask the universe, and the universe’s freedom in what answer we get (for example, the specific path or location of a measured particle).

David Bohm and Basil Hiley similarly spoke of [“pre-space” and “pre-physics.”](https://books.google.co.uk/books/about/Physics_and_the_Ultimate_Significance_of.html?id=9iXNm1ClYYQC&redir_esc=y) They developed a radical framework in which quantum mechanics and general relativity occupy what they called the “explicate order,” which is unfolded from a more abstract, non-spatiotemporal and immaterial “implicate order”—much as a symphony must be unfolded in space, time and matter from an abstract realm of composition in order to find its worldly expression. Again, while a physical world of space, time and matter is the goal, the foundation is something very different indeed—an abstract (non-commutative) algebraic structure, according to Hiley, whose representations (for example, some concrete realization of the algebraic structure in some objects and their motions) deliver the appropriate physics that we know and love (including [quantum theory and relativity, but also the mind](https://gerald-baron.medium.com/bohm-hileys-implicate-explicate-order-and-consciousness-07af6f506ef0) generated as a distinct, dual representation of the same underlying non-physical, non-mental structure). Again, how the algebraic structure is unfolded into a worldly (explicate) picture depends on contextual factors that must be established by an agent/participator of some kind.

Though there have, then, already been suggestions along the lines I am proposing, what has not been properly appreciated is where these approaches point: away from materialism, yes, but to what? This is obviously not quite orthodox physics, but neither does it match any of the orthodox metaphysical viewpoints. It is not materialist, but it is not [idealist](http://iai.tv/articles/quantum-physics-proves-a-conscious-universe-bruce-gordon-auid-2765) or [dualist](http://iai.tv/video/david-malone-in-depth-interview-dualism-consciousness) either. Like materialism and idealism, it is monistic: there is only one kind of reality. But it is a “dual-aspect” monism, according to which the fundamental stuff of reality—which is not spacetime, nor matter, nor mind—decomposes into different aspects: the spatiotemporal and material (the physical world); and the mental.

To accomplish this feat—a unitary structure to experience itself—we need the polarity provided by mind and world, a subject and an object. This splitting of a fundamental unitary structure into what we view as the world of experience (and therefore of physics) is deeply entangled with meaning, understood as the conversion of something immaterial into something with the appearance of concrete actuality, of a world. Yet neither mind nor matter pre-exist one another, and are firmly coupled, with each a sine qua non of the other. They are bound in a kind of self-generating, strange loop. The task for future physics, at least if it is to resolve the problem of quantum gravity, is to investigate what kind of psychophysically neutral substrate is capable of generating this bound pair of mind and matter, with the various laws and features we observe. We are led into new territory, since we must face [potential paradoxes of including ourselves, as active participators, in the system we are modelling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_6inIvk66mI&t=2772s).

Harald Atmanspacher and I outline this general view in great detail in [Dual-Aspect Monism and the Deep Structure of Meaning](https://www.routledge.com/Dual-Aspect-Monism-and-the-Deep-Structure-of-Meaning/Atmanspacher-Rickles/p/book/9781032219202?srsltid=AfmBOoqrhu0GI8EMt2PuGl4ScklcmAKzuhoAheVD74Hf1BWV-VnMpied). Bohm and Hiley’s implicate order and Wheeler’s immaterial-until-questioned realm both fit the bill to be a more ultimate reality, with the manifested physics connected with what might broadly be understood as “epistemic aspects.” But we have here a more general framework and methodology which is by no means exhausted by these two approaches (indeed, a [recent theory of Stephen Wolfram’s](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2021/11/the-concept-of-the-ruliad/) based on what he calls [“the Ruliad”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16068) also fits, as, I think, does the “[amplituhedron](https://brill.com/view/journals/time/12/2/article-p189_015.xml)” approach of Nima Arkani-Hamed and Donald Hoffman). It amounts to the task of finding a common neutral (with respect to space, time, matter, and mind) structure capable of generating the phenomena of quantum mechanics and general relativity and our experience of a world apparently described by these theories. The key requirement is to drop the idea that our usual categories of physics (of space, time, matter) are ultimate ones; instead, they must be described as aspects of a far more primitive domain. What remains is to explore this new frontier.
