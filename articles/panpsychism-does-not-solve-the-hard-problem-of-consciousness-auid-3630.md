# Panpsychism does not solve the hard problem of consciousness

*Inverted and multiplied problems of mind*

**By Tony Sobrado** | *24th July 2026* | *Read time: approx. 9 mins* | *1,767 words*

![Panpsychism does not solve the hard problem of consciousness](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/panpsychism-does-not-solve-the-hard-problem-of-consciousness.webp)

---

*Panpsychism has grown in popularity, and many now see it as the leading answer to the hard problem of consciousness – the problem of how consciousness emerges from physical matter. But here, **Tony Sobrado** argues panpsychism does not solve the hard problem of consciousness. Rather, panpsychism only inverts and multiplies the problems of consciousness. If matter arises out of some fundamental consciousness, we have a comparable emergence problem as the original hard problem. While we also create other problems, like the combination problem: the problem of how separate bits of conscious matter combine into a single entity?*

Panpsychism argues that consciousness does not emerge from the physical brain, nor is it caused by the physical brain. Panpsychism also denies that consciousness is realized in, or reducible to, the physical brain. The central grievance of panpsychists is treating physicalism and materialism as the fundamental layer of reality. This is because for panpsychists, it is not physicalism and materialism that are primary; instead, consciousness itself is the fundamental basis of reality. As a consequence, consciousness is not an emergent phenomenon but the very essence of existence itself. Instead of asking how subjective experience arises from non‑experiential physical matter like brains, panpsychism claims the opposite: that matter itself is composed of conscious units.

\_\_\_

Instead of explaining how consciousness emerges from possible physical transitions, panpsychism must explain how physical transitions emerge from consciousness.

\_\_\_

This inversion is meant to dissolve the hard problem of consciousness (how and why subjective experience coexists with physicalism and materialism) by making subjective experience the actual base of reality itself rather than a mere derivative. For panpsychists of all stripes, it is easier to get physical matter out of consciousness than it is to get consciousness out of physical matter. It should be noted that certain forms of panpsychism deny the ontology of physical matter in the first instance, in a pure idealist manner, and this will be a later focus.

Panpsychism, however, [does not solve the hard problem of consciousness](https://iai.tv/articles/bernardo-kastrup-why-panpsychism-is-baloney-auid-2214)—or any problem of consciousness—for all it does is firstly invert the traditional hard problem and then secondly multiply it. This is because once consciousness becomes the foundation of reality, new explanatory burdens arise concerning physical objectivity, individuation, transitional states, and the ontological status of the physical itself.

**How does panpsychism invert the hard problem of consciousness?**

There are two profound challenges encountered by panpsychism when it inverts the central question behind the hard problem of consciousness, which is *How consciousness arises from, or at least coexists with, physical matter*,because panpsychism must then explain how objective physical laws and individual conscious minds arise from a universe already innately saturated with consciousness.

The first of these issues is panpsychism’s objectivity problem: it fails to guarantee objective independent reality, which is the bedrock of the modern world, as crassly evidenced by flying planes. “Mind over matter” is a fun sticky poster and an endearing sentiment for mental positivity, but in actuality no evidence exists that anyone can bring down a plane by willing it so. There appears to be a closed domain to physical reality, a wall prohibiting mental interference. Newton’s law that the force of gravity (F) between two objects is directly proportional to the product of their masses cannot be willingly changed by a human mind.

If conscious sentience is the foundational base of reality from which all other layers stem, however, then everything in existence is on some level conscious, even in the simplest of forms. This then entails that every particle has some form of consciousness, sentience and mentality—however minimal. Consciousness and mentality, however, imply observations, subjective experience, memory, and perhaps most importantly, cognition, preference and will. An entity like a particle possessing conscious experience and a will immediately threatens the stability of physical law. If a conscious particle can *choose*, even in the faintest sense, not to follow a predictable pattern, let alone a physical law, then the universe should be completely chaotic. This is because particles are conceptually understood, both ontologically and epistemologically, within the framework of physical laws explicitly; and physical laws depend on stable, repeatable regularities. By contrast, consciousness is associated with non-regularity, dynamism and spontaneity. Panpsychism therefore faces a stark contradiction, either:

• Fundamental consciousness has a will, in which case physical laws and the independence of an external physical world cannot be instantiated (despite overwhelming evidence of an independent physical domain with predictable and constant parameters).

or

• Fundamental consciousness has no agency or will, in which case it is governed by strict deterministic laws that maintain physical order but limit conscious experience to being a purely passive passenger merely observing and experiencing with no subjective perspective or will.

As to the second option, if fundamental consciousness is governed by deterministic laws—laws that function like *a* + *b* = *c*, not only does this restrict an understanding of consciousness proper (this is inhibitory because remember panpsychism is attempting to solve the hard problem of consciousness, which means accounting for consciousness in all its glory like agency and will) but consciousness also becomes mechanistic, predictable and universally monolithic. This would be paradoxical because the base reality and all its composing units could not have any sort of individually distinct subjectivity if it is constrained by an ultimate determinism. At this point, therefore, calling it “consciousness” risks becoming a purely token concept as the very qualities that make consciousness uniquely meaningful, such as freedom, spontaneity and intentionality, are surrendered.

Panpsychism therefore cannot have both fundamental consciousness and physical objectivity without sacrificing one, either:

• Consciousness is the fundamental layer but physics becomes unstable.

or

• Physics is stable but consciousness becomes inert.

**Minimally conscious matter?**

Now some panpsychists argue that not literally everything in existence is conscious in a complete manner; rather, that [consciousness is simply woven into the primary fabric of the physical world in an elementary tacit way](https://iai.tv/video/metaphysics-vs-consciousness). The proposal here is that the most basic physical entities like fundamental particles possess extremely primitive, almost unimaginably simple forms of experience and not necessarily will and agency. Not only is this a conceptual issue already addressed above, but if consciousness is primitive with unimaginably simple forms of experience, and yet the ultimate universal existential base, then two more perplexing questions arise:

> 1. If the universe is fundamentally conscious at its base, but only minimally with no willing agency, then how does this progress to more elaborate forms of consciousness, i.e., *How do willing minds with preferences and choices come to be?*
>
> 2. If the universe is fundamentally conscious at its base, whether minimally or maximally, then how do *individual* consciousnesses emerge across the landscape of existence i.e., *Why are there multiple subjectivities rather than one vast, undifferentiated field of awareness and experience, and how does this occur*?

This is the meta problem of expansion and individuation faced by panpsychism. There is no structure or mechanism explaining how this relation and then separation occurs. That is, if consciousness is primal and primary with minimal experience, then how do “elevated” forms of consciousness with agency, will and general cognition emerge? In addition, if consciousness remains intrinsic and fundamental, but with either very simple experience or alternatively with full agency and will, then how does the primary underlying oneness of consciousness that encapsulates both notions here become multiple forms of individual subjective consciousness across our perceived reality? This produces a problem of *emergence* for panpsychism regardless. Ironically, the problem of emergence was [a problem panpsychism itself was trying to solve](https://iai.tv/video/materialism-and-emergence-cant-explain-consciousness-with-alex-oconnor), or avoid, by inverting the physicalist assumptions behind the traditional hard problem of consciousness.

To escape the challenges encompassing the status of objective physical reality, the nature of emergence and the structural mechanisms by which complex phenomena arise, certain panpsychist positions simply eliminate the physical altogether, or reinterpret the physical as nothing more than a phase‑state or structural expression of consciousness itself. This manoeuvre conveniently removes the need for causal mechanisms, production processes or explanatory structures since those belong to the domain of the natural scientific method and only apply if the physical world genuinely exists ontologically.

By denying physicalism outright, or presenting it as a structural extension of consciousness, these panpsychist positions claim that consciousness requires no formal or scientific “explanation” because the physical realm—where such causal mechanistic explanations would operate—does not actually exist. As already mentioned, this creates an obvious tension because the scientific method demonstrably works across every domain in which it is applied, with medicine and engineering being the most striking examples.

If you believe, moreover, that matter is a mere phase‑state of consciousness, then it is incumbent on the panpsychist to unveil paradigms and notions (not necessarily scientific theories) regarding said transitional states because we still need a coherent account of how consciousness transitions from one form to another. This is yet another inversion of the hard problem of consciousness. Instead of explaining how consciousness emerges from possible physical transitions, panpsychism must explain how physical transitions emerge from consciousness. Alternatively, if the physical is illusory, it becomes difficult to account for the extraordinary predictive and practical success of the sciences, and so an explanation of the *actual illusion* of the physical is also required.

**More problems than physicalism?**

As a result, under panpsychism, the fundamental challenges and questions posed by the hard problem of consciousness do not disappear. Instead, they are present in new inverted forms that just collapse back into the very same difficulties, in terms of conceptual asymmetry, that panpsychism was attempting to avoid:

• If physical matter exists but is a phase-state of consciousness (i.e. matter is a distinct stage of consciousness), then we need a coherent account of how consciousness transitions into a physical form. *This is an inversion of the general hard problem of consciousness i.e. how a physical object like a brain is capable of being sentient*.

• If matter is a stage of consciousness but not ontologically distinct, then they are one and the same, but then we require a theoretical framework for how and why there is still a phase-state distinction. *This is an inversion of identity theory, under physicalism, in the hard problem of consciousness.*

• If physical matter and consciousness are ontologically distinct, then panpsychism still faces [both a combination problem and an emergence problem](https://iai.tv/video/the-mystery-of-emergence). *These two challenges are already present in the traditional hard problem of consciousness.*

• If the physical does not ontologically exist yet there appears to be an independent physical reality, this produces the illusionist problem of panpsychism. Illusionism, under physicalism, is already conversely used to dissolve the hard problem of consciousness. *The inversion versus panpsychism is that phenomenal consciousness is the ontological illusion and not the physical.*

At best, panpsychism just inverts the hard problem of consciousness. At worst, it multiplies the original challenges by posing even more opaque questions.
