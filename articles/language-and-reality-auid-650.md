# Language and Reality

*Is meaning a dangerous human fantasy?*

**By Editorial** | *12th March 2016* | *Read time: approx. 1 mins* | *267 words*

![Language and Reality](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/Editorial-47.webp)

---

Perhaps responsible for the success of the human species, the power of language is remarkable. Yet a description of love, or a storm at sea, is not the same as the experience itself. Might language not describe reality at all? Is meaning a dangerous human fantasy of unlimited power, or can it tell us how things really are?  
  
 In this issue of IAI News, our contributors accept that there is a world out there that, in principle at least, we can understand and engage with. But the question is: how? Through language? We use language every day to communicate with others and [understand the world](http://iainews.iai.tv/articles/beyond-words-auid-648), says philosopher Emma Borg. So why do we now distrust it so much? Anthropologist Daniel Everett seeks to understand these problems from an evolutionary perspective. Could evolution explain [why language works](http://iainews.iai.tv/articles/the-kingdom-of-speech-auid-649)? And why it will always be limited?  
  
 Meanwhile, behavioural scientist Dylan Evans shifts the debate from language to imagination. If [our imaginations shape our reality](http://iainews.iai.tv/articles/imagining-reality-auid-646), he asks, do we need to conjure new visions of better worlds? Or will this impede real progress?  
  
 It is in this context that we see a return to religion. Sociologist Eileen Barker charts the [return of the pagans](http://iainews.iai.tv/articles/return-of-the-pagans-auid-645) while, much more worryingly, journalist John Lloyd issues a wake-up call to [the reality of terrorism](http://iainews.iai.tv/articles/terrorism-and-the-truth-auid-644) and the existential threats facing our culture and values.

And finally, former-clergyman, now writer and teacher Mark Vernon takes us back to Ancient Greece in search of goodness. We tend to think that goodness is a moral judgment, he says. Could Plato help us understand [the joys of virtue](http://iainews.iai.tv/articles/how-to-be-good-auid-647)?
