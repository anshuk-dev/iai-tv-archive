# Lionel Shriver: We live in an age of political mania

*It’s dangerous and socially self-destructive to join the resistance*

**By Lionel Shriver** | *20th August 2025* | *Read time: approx. 9 mins* | *1,874 words*

![Lionel Shriver: We live in an age of political mania](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/mania-in-society-1.webp)

---

*From #MeToo to Black Lives Matter, COVID lockdowns to climate change, modern political culture is increasingly driven by manias, so argues author of We Need To Talk About Kevin and political commentator Lionel Shriver. Shriver argues that these collective hysterias share a dangerous pattern: rational debate is shut down, dissent is punished, and conformity becomes a survival strategy. Shriver explores how intelligent societies can succumb to irrational movements, why labels like 'left' and 'right' have lost meaning, and why manias are not a partisan problem but a human one.*

**You’ve stated that your book *Mania* was inspired by recent social manias. Can you explain what these are and how they catch on in society?**

The [#MeToo movement](https://iai.tv/video/how-we-came-to-metoo) definitely had a manic element. It was striking partly because of how international it became and how quickly it got out of control. It started as a way for women with legitimate grievances to have their voices heard, but it rapidly became a requirement, as a female, to have a grievance and to have been abused in some way. What qualified as abuse began to spread until it included a bad date, for which men should be shunned and their careers ended.

The Black Lives Matter movement was established in 2013, if I recall correctly, but it was only in 2020 that it exploded. That was literally an overnight phenomenon. Within a day or two of George Floyd’s murder, you had people trooping by the tens of thousands down the street, even outside of the United States, where it had nothing to do with them. It was really disconcerting.

COVID definitely had a manic element. We got with the program way too fast. I was shocked by how readily a country like the UK simply rolled over and abdicated every civil right they had previously considered their birthright. Suddenly, it was perfectly normal for the government to tell you that you couldn’t leave your house. That’s just astonishing.

\_\_\_

You have a left that has become fundamentally authoritarian, constantly calling the other side “fascist,” when their own behavior often resembles fascism more closely.

\_\_\_

I think I would make a case for the fact that the [climate change](https://iai.tv/articles/we-still-dont-understand-climate-change-auid-2843) movement is also a mania. Whatever the actual science is, the fact that you are not allowed to differ or to amass evidence that contravenes this way of looking at the world and the future is a red flag for me. That, more than just a passing social interest, is what qualifies a mania: it has a religious component.**[![how to overcome groupthink](https://iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWyI0MDAiLCIyNzUiXQ/how-to-overcome-groupthink.webp)

**You’ve stated that manias often involve intelligent, rational people, like some scientists during COVID. How can you recognize a mania as it’s happening, rather than only in hindsight?**

By applying that test: during COVID, if you resisted the lockdowns—if you said, “This is not the way we have ever dealt with a contagious disease before, and it’s bound to do all kinds of economic damage, and closing the schools was a terrible idea”—you were not allowed to say that at the time. A whole generation is still paying the price for that. That means you’re caught up in something that is post-rational.

A legitimate perspective or policy admits criticism. People who are confident in their positions don't get hysterical when you differ; they simply respond because they feel secure in their reasoning and the evidence supporting it. They can say, “Go ahead, let’s have the conversation and I will refute you.” It’s not frightening to have the actual conversation, and they don’t resort to ad hominem. [During COVID](https://iai.tv/articles/creating-the-covid-19-story-auid-1529), it was all ad hominem. As soon as you open your mouth to say, “No, let’s not have another lockdown,” you’re a granny killer. The vaccination issue became manic, and not to mention vicious. I remember walking down the street with my next-door neighbor in Brooklyn in the summer of 2021. She was so angry and she said, “I hope that all the people who haven't gotten vaccinated die.” And she was serious. It wasn't a joke. That’s crazy, right? That’s part of what makes a mania: it's a little crazy.

**Is there a risk that labeling something a mania just becomes a way to dismiss opposing viewpoints? For example, how the lab-leak theory of COVID was once dismissed as a mania but is now considered a legitimate explanation?**

Of course, it’s just a word, and there are many synonyms—but those are just words, too. Using a label doesn’t get you out of having a discussion, defending your position, or disproving someone else’s. So I can say, “Well that’s just a mania,” and I haven't got anywhere. It’s a useful thumbnail for me because I have titled my book *Mania*, so I end up using it a lot just in discussing the book. But I don’t think slapping labels on people's positions gets you anywhere.

\_\_\_

It’s often irrational to resist what everyone else at least claims to believe. In fact, I even submit somewhere in the book that it may be to your evolutionary advantage to be a conformist.

**Do you think one side of the political aisle is more prone to mania than the other?**

Right now, clearly the left. It’s not that the left is more irrational than the right, though I think they are a bit more irrational right now. That said, I’m not entirely comfortable with the [left-right terminology](https://iai.tv/articles/the-age-of-left-right-politics-is-over-auid-2993). It's very confused right now. You have a left that has become fundamentally authoritarian, constantly calling the other side “fascist,” when their own behavior often resembles fascism more closely. These words have become completely meaningless.

The reason that the sequence of manias I have identified all pretty much [lie on the left](https://iai.tv/articles/rethinking-the-left-susan-neiman-auid-2564) is that the left has been in control. They’ve had the cultural upper hand for decades—dominating the media, publishing, and until recently, winning plenty of elections. They dominate education, which is huge. Not that left-wing positions are necessarily prone to psychic contagion. I don't think it’s a partisan problem, it’s a species problem.

**So, do you think manias arise more from people being corrupted by power?**

No. They arise from people being fearful.

When the word goes out that you may not contest a particular perspective, there may be serious consequences if you do—risking your career, your reputation, even your personal relationships. So most sensible people keep their mouths shut. We’ve seen that a lot during the last dozen years. And I’m sympathetic. A lot of people say, “Oh, you're so brave.” Well, maybe I'm just kind of stupid and self-destructive, like my main character in *Mania*. I wrote that wittingly—she finally explodes in a circumstance that doesn't do her any good and does her family tremendous harm. It’s irrational.

It’s often irrational to resist what everyone else at least claims to believe. In fact, I even submit somewhere in the book that it may be to your evolutionary advantage to be a conformist. Take Nazism: if you kept your mouth shut or you went along with it, then you didn't end up in prison or in a concentration camp. You survived. If you want to pass on your genes, which is what's in your interest evolutionarily, then you get with the program because it’s dangerous and socially self-destructive to join the resistance until the resistance is seriously underway.

**You’ve outlined manias that are specific to the left, but some say that we should be worried about those specific to the right. For example: Trump sending out lists of words to be banned by federal agencies, Daryl Cooper calling Churchill the real villain of World War II, or the 2020 election denialism about Donald Trump. Do you think the right are just as vulnerable to such mania?**

Oh, totally. I mean to say more broadly, because we can get hung up on mania and what defines it. All humans are vulnerable to irrationality, emotionalism, and most of all, tribalism. Certainly the Trump stuff is deeply tribal. I don’t approve of Trump, or anybody, submitting a list of words that you’re not allowed to use. That’s what the left has been doing. As a writer, talker, and person, I resent that. I find that an enormous imposition on me. I have a list of words that I voluntarily refuse to use because I hate them. I never use “person of color” without quotation marks because I think it’s a dumb expression. But I don't want to be told that I have to stop using it. Maybe I'll start using “person of color” once they stop using it. I hate that kind of linguistic fascism, if you will.

\_\_\_

I don’t think that any of the things that we’re talking about are exclusive to a political position. It has to do with people being a political species.

\_\_\_

**So it seems you disagree with the view that the left’s manias are fading. That with the Supreme Court’s recent rulings, Trump’s election, and the rise of right-wing parties in Europe and the UK, the right is really the ascendant force.**

I think it’s more that they’re lying low, which is different from going away. All the people who hold “woke” opinions are still in positions of power and influence. They control the charity sector, the museum sector, the arts, most of the media, and the universities. So what’s changed?

I’m hopeful that Trump’s election means turning a corner. And for the record, I’ve never been a Trump supporter. A lot of people make that mistake. I like some of the things that he’s done, I don’t like others. I think he did lose the 2020 election. I wasn’t too keen on January 6th. Though trying to strike a middle course in relation to Trump is practically impossible—you’re either in one camp or another and that’s a difficult place for me to maintain. But I don’t think that any of the things that we’re talking about are exclusive to a political position. It has to do with people being a political species. We operate in groups. That’s where I’m personally alienated from my own kind because I don’t like groups. I’ve never been a joiner, it’s one of the reasons I do what I do for a living. I’m a loner. Which is why I find it mystifying that most of my fellow writers have got with the woke program 100%. You would think they would be loners also.

**You mentioned earlier that the traditional left-right dichotomy doesn’t really apply any longer. Do you think we need a new dichotomy to talk about politics? And what might that be?**

Honestly, I don’t know. The reason that we end up using those terms is that we don’t have anything else. There are adjectives, but they’re still referring to the same axis. For example, I find it completely arbitrary that the left ended up, after October 7th, suddenly celebrating Palestine and Palestinians and complaining about Israel, even before [Israel had started to retaliate.](https://iai.tv/articles/only-states-can-wage-war-and-why-it-matters-auid-2923)

**Well, some parts of the left were horrified by the violence and firmly against the war.**

Yes. Good for them. I didn’t think there was any excuse for not being universally horrified by that assault. When you know anything about the details—see Douglas Murray—it’s astonishing that human beings are capable of doing that. I tend to be pretty cynical about human nature, so it’s hard to surprise me, but I was genuinely surprised. So, I find any other reaction to that mystifying.
