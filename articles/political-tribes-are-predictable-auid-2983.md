# Political tribes are predictable

*Do childhood traits decide your politics?*

**By John T Jost** | *28th October 2024* | *Read time: approx. 13 mins* | *2,543 words*

![Political tribes are predictable](https://iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWyI4MDAiLCI1MDAiXQ/24.10.24.-ideological-origin-jtm-copy.webp)

---

*With the US election imminent, we view who to vote for as one of the most important choices in our lives.  But, writes John T. Jost, whether liberal or conservative, our political affinities as adults can be predicted from early childhood. American and global studies show that temperament and psychological traits such as threat sensitivity correlate with ideological divides. A mix of nature and nurture means our political beliefs are at least partly heritable – though not inevitable.*

Gilbert and Sullivan’s comic opera *Iolanthe* included the celebrated [refrain](https://www.gsarchive.net/iolanthe/iollib.pdf) ‘Every boy and every gal, That’s born into the world alive, Is either a little Liberal, Or else a little Conservative!’ There is, as it turns out, a grain of truth in this one-hundred-and-sixty-year-old song. It is certainly not the case that people are destined to occupy the political left, right, or center in any inevitable sense, but events that are set into motion in childhood, adolescence, and beyond do indeed shape our political orientations, whether we like to admit it or not.

Sigmund Freud believed that our basic personalities are more or less settled by the age of five. Contemporary research psychologists would probably not put such a fine point on it, but there can be little doubt that early experiences create path dependence, shaping subsequent developments, which create a kind of snowball effect in which correlated traits accumulate and manifest themselves in all areas of human behavior, including social and political behavior. The ontogenetic origins of our ideological orientations, in other words, begin in childhood, as vague hints and traces that, through subsequent experiences and social interactions, develop much later into full-fledged convictions for which people, in some cases, are willing to risk their lives—and the lives of others—for, as pro-Trump insurrectionists did on January 6, 2021, in Washington, DC.

Let us begin at the beginning. Between 1969 and 1971, conjugal scientific collaborators, Jack and Jeanne Block, visited two nursery schools in Berkeley and Oakland, CA, and asked a group of 6 teachers to provide independent evaluations of the cognitive, social, and personality characteristics of each of the 3- and 4-year-olds under their care. The researchers pooled the 6 teacher evaluations, which were in concordance with one another, to estimate the children’s traits, and then stayed in touch with the families for 20 years.

Around 1989, when the kids turned 23 years of age, 95 of them (from an initial group of 128) completed surveys that included lots of questions about their political beliefs, opinions, and values. [The researchers discovered](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656605000632?via%3Dihub) that the boys and girls who were rated by their nursery schoolteachers as more independent, self-reliant, resourceful, and confident turned out to hold more liberal or progressive attitudes at age 23, whereas the children who were rated as more anxious, guilty, and stressed expressed more conservative attitudes at age 23. Many people, even in psychology, found it shocking that childhood temperament could predict political orientation in adulthood.

This study, which was touted by the *Toronto* Star, was especially notable because it cohered with the results of a [meta-analysis](https://psycnet.apa.org/buy/2003-00782-003) of international studies based on adults that my colleagues and I had published just a few years before. In that research, we found that individuals who experienced uncertainty and ambiguity as aversive and were sensitive to potential threats in their environment were more drawn to conservative-rightist (vs. liberal-leftist) beliefs, opinions, and values. However, skeptics objected that the Block and Block study was conducted in Northern California, which raised the possibility that future liberals (or the children of liberal parents) were more psychologically secure because they were socially validated in such a progressive environment. One well-known psychologist went so far as to declare publicly that the childhood study was ‘biased, shoddy work, poor science at best.’

In 1991, more than seven hundred families from Arkansas, California, Kansas, Massachusetts, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin were studied when their newborn children were only 1 month old and then followed for the next 18 years. When the children were 4 and a half years old, the mothers completed a series of questionnaires about their parenting attitudes and their children’s behavior, and the children, upon reaching adulthood in 2009, answered questions about their own political attitudes.

\_\_\_

The fact that anything about one’s childhood predicts ideological preferences 30 or more years later amazes many people, and rightly so.

\_\_\_

[The researchers who analyzed all of the data discovered](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797612440102?casa_token=66UnMwfG2zQAAAAA%3AKvz8MwS_YxK4k2zAj2cSoDTsVTgH-7tug8AvtDSMP7CqvFTE4fI9Luv-3IW4B-HakpZ9wLaX6MhHWA&journalCode=pssa) that mothers who endorsed egalitarian childrearing in the first month of parenthood—agreeing, for instance, that ‘A child’s ideas should be seriously considered in making family decisions’—had children who were more liberal in young adulthood. On the other hand, mothers who endorsed authoritarian childrearing in 1991—believing that ‘The most important thing to teach children is absolute obedience to whoever is in authority’—had children who were more conservative in 2009. These findings are clearly in line with observations going back [more than 50 years](https://www.amazon.com/Radical-Man-Process-Psycho-Social-Development/dp/B000KW5ZBC/ref=sr_1_1?crid=2XGWVZNA6E7AL&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.NkZkvrc3SG1nSMJuML4tZdZtvkl5DFNhG23XEqe4LEHGjHj071QN20LucGBJIEps.Rl3zv3ANQmeBu-mpGmUhc4FGQebN8JYbAtnA9WJSv3w&dib_tag=se&keywords=hampden-turner+radical+man&qid=1725297563&sprefix=hampden-turner+radical+man%2Caps%2C120&sr=8-1) that there is a genuine psychological connection between authoritarianism and political conservatism.

When the researchers adjusted statistically for demographic factors, they also discovered that children with higher levels of activity, restlessness, and attentional focusing at 54 months [were more liberal](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797612440102?casa_token=66UnMwfG2zQAAAAA%3AKvz8MwS_YxK4k2zAj2cSoDTsVTgH-7tug8AvtDSMP7CqvFTE4fI9Luv-3IW4B-HakpZ9wLaX6MhHWA&journalCode=pssa) at 18 years of age, whereas children with higher levels of fearfulness turned out to be more conservative. Another group of researchers followed the [same cohort](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2020-31139-001) for another 8 years. They found that all of the above correlations between childhood temperament and political orientation persisted to the age of 26 and that children who displayed an anxious attachment style (fearing abandonment and reflecting insecurity) at age 3 were more likely to grow up conservative, whereas children who displayed avoidant attachment (preferring independence over emotional closeness) were more likely to grow up liberal. Thus, the basic findings from the controversial Northern California study were eventually replicated throughout the U.S.

Another group of researchers analyzed data from a [longitudinal study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jopy.12874) of more than 4,000 people born in 1970 in England, Scotland, or Wales. Their findings were much weaker and confined to social (as opposed to economic) conservatism. In the UK, children who, according to their parents, exhibited more negativity at ages 5 and 10 turned out to be more socially (but less economically) conservative at ages 26 and 42. Although correlations between childhood temperament and adult political orientation are considerably (and unsurprisingly) weaker than correlations between [adult personality and political orientation](https://www.amazon.com/Left-Right-Psychological-Significance-Distinction/dp/0190858338/ref=sr_1_1?crid=1P14CKZFLKMZR&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.X6L4fZcExReb3EVHkimTTPhcrPWTLZaFccMc6ieECBfGjHj071QN20LucGBJIEps.grVDPFT9QZ8lg3MntkluqnMN50zyWuhy-MV4iYGiOtE&dib_tag=se&keywords=jost+left+right&qid=1725297809&sprefix=jost+left+right%2Caps%2C96&sr=8-1), the fact that *anything* about one’s childhood predicts ideological preferences 30 or more years later amazes many people, and rightly so.

Social scientists today assume that left-right (or, in the U.S., liberal-conservative) political orientation, like virtually every other interesting human characteristic, is a function of *both* nature and nurture. We know that the political attitudes of parents and offspring are positively correlated in families in which children are adopted—but they are [even more strongly correlated](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/09567976211021844?casa_token=MPlcz0OBvhYAAAAA%3Arz3dwOaewWoDguLX6up49q_Lcp0QWb69orabS8CA0C1liUJiEWeVMd0HIIs7Ccxkc0ErMxPg7lY-BQ&journalCode=pssa) in families that are biologically related. Studies comparing monozygotic vs. dizygotic twins in Australia, Canada, Denmark, England, Germany, Hungary, Poland, Sweden, and the U.S. suggest that 40 to 50% of the statistical variability in political orientation is attributable to [genetically heritable factors](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352154620300553). There is also suggestive evidence from the [U.K.](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(11)00289-2?fbclid=IwAR12tRePyr9PJWItAnQAgreZLEIHaI0sjlb0_9yFuOjUjDthb1H9OCxwwTU), [U.S.](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn1979), and [Netherlands](https://www.cell.com/iscience/fulltext/S2589-0042(24)01757-7?os=roku&ref=app) that there are small but significant differences between liberal-leftists and conservative-rightists in both brain structures and functions.

Although there are skeptics who claim that, because of the phenomenon of [pleiotropy](https://www.nature.com/scitable/topicpage/pleiotropy-one-gene-can-affect-multiple-traits-569/), genes could independently influence psychological and ideological outcomes [without any causal connection](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0118106) between the two, most researchers today believe that psychological characteristics that are partially heritable, such as cognitive abilities, temperamental factors, personality traits, and orientations toward uncertainty and threat, mediate (and moderate) the effects of genes on political orientation. Although there is no “left” or “right” gene, there seem to be [genetic origins of attitudinal preferences](https://psycnet.apa.org/buy/2011-22899-001) for equality and social progress, which predict liberal-leftist views, as well as preferences for tradition and social hierarchy, which predict conservative-rightist views.

None of this is to deny the obvious fact that social environments are critical for the development of political orientation. Back in the 1930s, Theodore Newcomb [demonstrated](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Political_Attitudes_Over_the_Life_Span/3a927Jder6IC?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=newcomb+bennington+college+study&pg=PR11&printsec=frontcover) that daughters of wealthy conservative Americans who attended the liberal Vermont college, Bennington, became—and stayed for decades—far more progressive than their parents. Conversely, young people who enter conservative-leaning institutions—such as religious or military colleges—tend to [move significantly to the right](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1009662807702) during the course of their education. Presumably these political and psychological shifts occur subtly, in thousands of tiny ways that unfold over the course of several years.

\_\_\_

In terms of overarching personality traits, liberal-leftists are more curious, creative, and open to new experiences, whereas conservative-rightists are more dutiful, rule-bound, and conscientious.

\_\_\_

            My own view, developed at length in the book [*Left & Right: The Psychological Significance of a Political Distinction*](https://www.amazon.com/Left-Right-Psychological-Significance-Distinction/dp/0190858338/ref=sr_1_1?crid=1P14CKZFLKMZR&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.X6L4fZcExReb3EVHkimTTPhcrPWTLZaFccMc6ieECBfGjHj071QN20LucGBJIEps.grVDPFT9QZ8lg3MntkluqnMN50zyWuhy-MV4iYGiOtE&dib_tag=se&keywords=jost+left+right&qid=1725297809&sprefix=jost+left+right%2Caps%2C96&sr=8-1), is that there are a number of “elective affinities”—akin to chemical forces of magnetic attraction or repulsion—between various psychological orientations, which are the joint product of nature and nurture, and ideological outcomes that, since at least the time of the French Revolution, can be located on a left-right dimension. For example, people who are more tolerant of ambiguity, uncertainty, and complexity are more likely to be progressive and egalitarian, and therefore leftist, and so are people who are more cognitively complex, deliberative, and less dogmatic. In terms of overarching personality traits, liberal-leftists are more curious, creative, and open to new experiences, whereas conservative-rightists are more dutiful, rule-bound, and conscientious. There are now hundreds of studies carried out all over the world that document these elective affinities, which vary slightly from country to country and much more widely from world region to world region. Although the effects are larger in Western, industrialized, democratic societies, they are present to a remarkable, yet diminished extent even in such countries as [Singapore, Pakistan, Tunisia, Rwanda, and the Philippines](https://osf.io/hmk5a).

            As obvious heirs to the Scientific Enlightenment, liberal-leftists are on more solid epistemic grounds than conservative-rightists. Research finds that the latter are more susceptible to [false rumors](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/01461672221114222?casa_token=GCqIto6eLKgAAAAA%3A_BgIXmHGVl_vpcybDKCzAZVJffIsw0iuXvYdtR2h4blbJEQVx846byL7aFswfVmGoO6sCE_zW7wfTw), [political misinformation](https://journalqd.org/article/view/2586), and [baseless conspiracy theories](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pops.12681), as we have seen vividly with respect to the [international spread of QAnon](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/audio/2020/sep/29/the-dangerous-global-spread-of-the-qanon-conspiracy-theory) and [popular support for Donald Trump’s bogus claims about election fraud in 2020](https://www.prri.org/spotlight/after-three-years-and-many-indictments-the-big-lie-that-led-to-the-january-6th-insurrection-is-still-believed-by-most-republicans/). But this does not mean the former are psychologically advantaged in other ways. Liberal-leftists are far more worried than conservative-rightists about social injustice, economic inequality, and climate change, and this apparently makes them [less happy](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02124.x?casa_token=_38_EHeP1_AAAAAA%3ADvK74Sb_NjJL7JjXJbzVUv84gM4X3NJ4byIskrrJBXBZ7Nw5XH9YafU-dJR2aE-_XNV-V70gaS7NhQ), [more anxious](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ijop.12796), and [more depressed](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666560321000438#bib58).

Wittgenstein and how to debate your enemy
By Drew Douglas Johnson](https://iai.tv/articles/wittgenstein-and-how-to-debate-your-enemy-drew-douglas-johnson-auid-2829)**

            There are skeptics and critics in every field, and [political psychology is no exception](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pops.12958). In addition to the authors noted above who believe that genetically heritable links between psychological and political variables are purely coincidental, there are also some who believe that correlations between psychological and political variables are [too small to be meaningful](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1047840X.2022.2065130?casa_token=uEpnL6frdHEAAAAA:YzHXM0hMlK3M2oiKxFA_dyq-3TLWV_wp2RpAKI2rm2ou_1xPqhWn5Bt9QQGa_Sino1hFaMjLIYs9eg&casa_token=2XhXkXdiO0oAAAAA:yrzTOsa0fJ_YVzM4h49cqrgjKbP-Pru8MkKefNysesyEnz__9cL4GnOgnWbq4ut43yFDtp5FJ8evuw). But they are [wrong](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1047840X.2022.2065135?casa_token=qxqfAT10KXIAAAAA%3AEkq5scT7nVCtVDmDgBPovFQr82NmSRdJsrEcjJwBKVaCdNCUoRwYevEKiGMtwTW-W4-ZKgSemV6WoQ). It is true that many of the individual correlations between psychological characteristics and ideological preferences are fairly small in magnitude (under *r* = .20), but some are in fact [quite large](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/figure/10.1080/1047840X.2022.2065135?scroll=top&needAccess=true). Furthermore, when you consider the cumulative effect of many small affinities it is possible to predict with a reasonably high degree of certainty any given individual’s placement on the left-right continuum.

For example, in ongoing research conducted in Bolsonaro’s Brazil, my colleagues and I have observed that a suite of epistemic variables, including dogmatism and intolerance of ambiguity and uncertainty predicted conservative-rightist preferences, just as they do in the U.S. and Europe. So did existential variables such as self-deception and perceptions of a dangerous world, and relational variables such as national identification. When these variables were entered into a multiple regression analysis, we were able to explain over 20% of the statistical variability in Brazilians’ left-right political orientation. When we included demographic as well as psychological factors in the model, we explained over 30% of the variability in political orientation.

            Other skeptics point out that cross-sectional, correlational studies, which are cheap to conduct and therefore plentiful in the research literature, make it is [impossible to draw any causal conclusions](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pops.12958) about how psychological predispositions shape ideological preferences over time. This is true. The glass is half-full if you ignore all of the longitudinal evidence, including the prospective studies of children I have described in this article, as well as [experimental and quasi-experimental evidence](https://www.amazon.com/Left-Right-Psychological-Significance-Distinction/dp/0190858338/ref=sr_1_1?crid=1P14CKZFLKMZR&dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.X6L4fZcExReb3EVHkimTTPhcrPWTLZaFccMc6ieECBfGjHj071QN20LucGBJIEps.grVDPFT9QZ8lg3MntkluqnMN50zyWuhy-MV4iYGiOtE&dib_tag=se&keywords=jost+left+right&qid=1725297809&sprefix=jost+left+right%2Caps%2C96&sr=8-1).

Highlighting the fact that ideological preferences are shaped by *nurture* as well as nature, there are by now dozens of studies showing that [exposure to terrorism leads individuals and communities to exhibit “conservative shift.”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ajps.12692) Less intuitively, when experimental social psychologists make people feel [rushed](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1559-1816.1974.tb02605.x?casa_token=FdJr-IktX3QAAAAA:_ZLAPd8cqAgW6OjOl-mz6TOw6Vp951Lgl3D7rRgYM75d-AbaeKKeUK9i9cybioZ_ChifCrfZstPZCQo) or [mentally overwhelmed](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0146167215591961?casa_token=hdn3Th4WLS0AAAAA%3AYyBRB22WCft_ghIwwVfgrrk4a8rxLAZMc5vhKL8qFGm1cPzEjAjOM_BmY1m1yr5xOzLBJuBbwdLpJw)—or [drunk](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0146167212439213?casa_token=Y2AnCfXLFm4AAAAA%3AZd89WLCEo4B06njKImNS8dytJYwnJ1HNIfJJ_W0CqtqDpqfFicNlhKQhllbTkTYB-AfDVkvErYyh1w)—they find that those people express more conservative-rightist beliefs, opinions, and values, compared to baseline. In one experiment, Stanford University students who were asked to ponder the role of chance, opportunity, and help from others—rather than discipline, hard work, and personal wisdom—in their academic success [expressed more liberal attitudes](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103109000882?casa_token=wu83iUwx9gsAAAAA:_EphMds6_47nLZPtqBx5Yb5zlh-6NfkoDqnysv6IcgmokXhvg-UgOvYgEwz88UsFDNkT5zWa).

            It is worth considering why, after hundreds of studies carried out all over the world over several decades, there are still so many people who are skeptical about the very [modest scientific proposal](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103109000882?casa_token=wu83iUwx9gsAAAAA:_EphMds6_47nLZPtqBx5Yb5zlh-6NfkoDqnysv6IcgmokXhvg-UgOvYgEwz88UsFDNkT5zWa) that*,* for psychological reasons emanating from nature and nurture, some individuals in late adolescence and early adulthood gravitate toward the liberal-left pole, whereas others gravitate toward the conservative-right (assuming, of course, they are exposed to an informational environment that includes some of both). Perhaps this is a scary idea to some because it implies that the basis for our ideological commitments is not entirely rational. What if many of our most cherished beliefs are derived from hopes, fears, needs, desires, and distortions—and not just logic and evidence?

A divided South Africa needs Arendt
By Thiven Reddy](https://iai.tv/articles/a-divided-south-africa-needs-arendt-auid-2851)**

            Some authors have suggested that liberal-leftists and conservative-rightists are more likely to [respect and tolerate each other’s differences if they recognize that such differences are biologically grounded and instantiated](https://www.amazon.com/Predisposed-Liberals-Conservatives-Political-Differences/dp/0415535875). The scientific evidence that emerged twenty or so years ago that sexual orientation has a genetic component may indeed have contributed to an [overall reduction in sexual prejudice](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1368430206059863?casa_token=DazpYmsovT4AAAAA:G9fbPizdsvHM0Gx2oJ6-1-zr36C2h4li39XSniEJyL-Qy4fbR-04uaAGFKOMJgumXM2MVvm_mtfI7Q). However, in the present era of political polarization, I fear the opposite is more likely—that old arguments for eugenics modeled on racism could resurface, especially on the far right. This does not mean that we should abandon the scientific exploration of personal and social origins of political orientation, for ignorance gets us nowhere, and willful ignorance gets us nowhere fast. Nor should we “essentialize” left-right differences or assume that they are immutable; we should merely recognize that ideology—like other features of human behavior—is a joint product of nature and nurture, and that all of us are potentially vulnerable to political manipulation of one kind or another.

As the philosopher [Peter Railton](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Morality_Reflection_and_Ideology/eyopmEJYjxYC?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=peter+railton+duck+sits+yet&pg=PA113&printsec=frontcover) has pointed out, there is something about ideological analysis that is inherently destabilizing for the believer, insofar as it “unmasks” the non-rational origins of their beliefs. People do not like [scientific explanations for their own political and religious commitments.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2005.01621.x) No one wants to sit next to a psychologist on an airplane, but maybe they would learn some interesting things if they did. Ego-defensiveness—especially when combined with group-defensiveness about our political “tribe” and system-defensiveness about established, culturally familiar ways of living and working—may help to explain why so many people—within the scientific community and without—continue to resist, often strenuously and sometimes hysterically, the accumulating evidence from political psychology that our choices about whom and what to believe are constrained by the lives we have led thus far. But some people do change their political beliefs in adulthood, often because of education or travel or romantic relationships or experiences with terrorism or war. Tomorrow, as we are fond of reminding ourselves, is another day.
