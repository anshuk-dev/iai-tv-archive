# Why maths alone cannot predict the economy

*Mathematical proof is not economic fact*

**By Matthew Watson** | *17th October 2024* | *Read time: approx. 8 mins* | *1,543 words*

![Why maths alone cannot predict the economy](https://iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWyI4MDAiLCI1MDAiXQ/Mathematical-Market-Models-versus-Lived-Economic-Reality.webp)

---

*To most people, the predictions of economists can be spoken of in the same breath as astrology or tarot readings. But, what is going on behind the scenes that leads to such flawed thinking? To a rarified and powerful section of economists, mathematical market models have been used to explain all behavioural characteristics and find higher-level truths that capture the essential elements of human life. [However, Matthew Watson argues](https://www.agendapub.com/page/detail/false-prophets-of-economics-imperialism/?k=9781788217668//), this treats abstract mathematical proof as empirical fact. Instead, we need to embrace a variety of methods in the social sciences and dethrone the abstract mathematical modellers if we are to find a better approach to the economy. This work is based upon his ESRC Professorial Fellowship project* [‘Rethinking the Market’](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/pais/research/completedprojects/rethinkingthemarket).

In his *General Theory* of 1936, John Maynard Keynes reflected on the power of the “ideas of economists” and famously concluded that the “world is ruled by little else”.  He complained that decision makers in all walks of life were “distilling their frenzy from some academic scribbler of a few years back”.  The ideas of economists are no less important today.  But in the place of Keynes’s scribblers we now have hypothetical mathematical modellers.  Wherever we turn, there seems to be a mathematical market model to guide us through our lives, everything from how we should balance work with leisure to who it would be a good idea to fall in love with.  If we cast our minds further afield to how our societies are governed, again there will always be a mathematical market model on hand to tell us what we need to know, [in the fields of politics, law and culture](http://iai.tv/articles/why-economics-needs-philosophy-auid-2757) every bit as much as about the economy.  The phenomenon of *economics imperialism* is evident whenever a mathematical market model is used to present an opinion on a social issue which, at heart, is neither a mathematical nor a market problem.  In this way, economics seeks new intellectual terrain to colonise, hoping to render other social sciences subordinate to its way of thinking.

\_\_\_

They have ridden on the coat tails of the abstract proofers, seeking to establish epistemic hierarchies in which they can claim to have arrived at higher-level truths that are unavailable to other social scientists, simply because they exhibit a mathematical frame of mind.

\_\_\_

However, this is not what the pioneers of mathematical economics had in mind.  When this mode of thinking came to prominence in the mid-twentieth century, its most important examples were exercises in proof-making, taking the most general case imaginable and working through it in a purely formal manner.  The resulting mathematical artefacts were forbidding in the extreme and demanded a level of technical skill that only a handful possessed.  They were entirely unreadable to the vast majority of economists, who had to make do instead with creating much simpler mathematical market models.  Today, the techniques involved in this latter endeavour often remain daunting for other social scientists, but there the similarities end.  The highly sophisticated proof-makers were always clear that they were engaged in overt thought experiments, imagining onto the page a state of affairs that conformed to no known world.  Economics imperialists, by contrast, insist that they are engaged in causal explanation, revealing to us the contours of the actual social world we inhabit.  They have ridden on the coat tails of the abstract proofers, seeking to establish epistemic hierarchies in which they can claim to have arrived at higher-level truths that are unavailable to other social scientists, simply because they exhibit a mathematical frame of mind.

It is one of the ironies of *economics imperialism* that while its most vocal proponents have been tub-thumpers in the extreme, its successes in colonising other social scientific fields have been enacted by the massed ranks of silent practitioners. For every person who has consciously sought to mobilise others to the flag of *economics imperialism*, hundreds more were quietly going about their business of populating academic journals with studies based on mathematical market models. They have needed no invitation from self-assured allies to present the purely logical implications of their models’ assumptive base as direct substitutes for the nuanced and contextually sensitive empirical analyses that underpin the work of the subject specialist social scientists.  Instead, they simply put their noses to the grindstone to rack up long lists of publications, because all they needed to show is that it was possible for the same mathematical market models to be applied to any social situation.

\_\_\_

Within social science it is usual to think of how any observation permits multiple causal stories, but if the economics imperialists present their mathematical market models as general cases, then they can turn them into single truths.

\_\_\_

Yet we should be clear what has happened here to the practice of causal observation.  No fully-functioning market system has ever presented itself in a directly observable manner, so to begin to think of how such a system might operate it is necessary to construct a [purely mental artefact to assist the reasoning process.](http://iai.tv/articles/who-really-owns-the-worlds-wealth-and-what-to-do-about-it-auid-2957)  This is what the mathematical proof-makers have done.  However, in my new book, [*False Prophets of Economics Imperialism*](https://www.agendapub.com/page/detail/false-prophets-of-economics-imperialism/?k=9781788217668//), I suggest that something else entirely is now under way.

Instead of allowing the purely mental artefact to reveal the limited transferability of its findings to the world beyond the model, economics imperialists typically prey on other social scientists’ anxiety about the limits of their own mathematical competence to collapse the space between the two.  Within social science it is usual to think of how any observation permits multiple causal stories, but if the economics imperialists present their mathematical market models as general cases, then they can turn them into single truths.  No direct observation of the world beyond the model is required anymore, because the ultimate arbiter of causality rests solely within the model world.  The social science instinct to go out into the actual world, look at it, and think about what it might be telling us is therefore rendered redundant.

In this way, economics imperialists must first transform the nature of the lived social relationships under study into ideal-typical market relationships.  They turn *our* world, the one we actually inhabit, into an alternative reality purely of their own creation.  Yet this amounts to no more than understanding the implications of having first assumed a range of completely unrealistic behavioural characteristics and then proceeding as if everyone had the capacity to be that type of person in practice.  No matter how hard you try, though, actual flesh and blood people cannot be required to act like a pure conceptual abstraction; any model that expects such behaviour will inevitably be flawed when it comes to causal explanation.  There are two different forms of knowledge in existence here, doing completely different things.

\_\_\_

The dividing line between these different approaches is located in new answers to the question of whether mathematical market models need corroboration from studies of real-world agents before they can be said to display genuine epistemic worth.

\_\_\_

As my book shows, the form of knowledge preferred by the economics imperialists is based on the twentieth century’s long escape from the empirical in economic theory.  It is this that allows them to present studies in logic as if they *were* empirical findings.  Mathematical instincts came to economic theory long before mathematical arguments, and longer still before those arguments were presented using formal mathematical notation.  The dividing line between these different approaches is located in new answers to the question of whether mathematical market models need corroboration from studies of real-world agents before they can be said to display genuine epistemic worth.  How closely, in other words, should we be able to see our own lives reflected back to us in the decisions that the agents make in the world within the model?

Contemporary economics imperialists typically proceed by simply placing their market template over every social context before passing off the resulting logical implications as empirical findings.  In other words, they dodge the all-important question of how accurately their models capture the corresponding social reality by treating their mathematical solutions as if they were actual empirical findings.  [What kind of philosophical justification exists for such an elision, though, is never made clear.](https://iai.tv/articles/the-fantasy-economics-of-the-nobel-prize-auid-2279)

[The way ahead cannot involve merely asking economics imperialists to no longer practice *economics imperialism*, because that ship has already long since sailed](https://iai.tv/articles/the-fantasy-economics-of-the-nobel-prize-auid-2279).  Instead, it resides in other social scientists displaying more confidence in their own work.  They are not in danger of their socially-situated studies of individuals in action completely disappearing under survival-of-the-fittest pressures.  As such, there is no associated need to feel apologetic about not being highly skilled mathematicians.  Economics imperialists will always be able to impose some such pecking order over non-mathematically oriented social scientists, but there will also always be a need for other forms of knowledge too.  Contextually-sensitive research into the behaviour of flesh and blood individuals will never be replaced by asking how an abstract market agent would be expected to act if the complex layering of social reality were to be replaced by a pristine market environment.  Epistemic worth exists in each mode of analysis, but it is of an entirely different nature.  Social scientists who operate on a non-mathematical terrain should feel emboldened to step out of the shadow cast by *economics imperialism* to celebrate the continued value of their work.
