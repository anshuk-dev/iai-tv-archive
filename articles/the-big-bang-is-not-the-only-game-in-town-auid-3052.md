# The Big Bang theory is not the only game in town

*The universe may be eternal*

**By Martín López Corredoira** | *20th January 2025* | *Read time: approx. 8 mins* | *1,518 words*

![The Big Bang theory is not the only game in town](https://cdn.iai.tv/assets/Uploads/_resampled/FillWzEyODAsNzIwXQ/big-bang.webp)

---

*The Big Bang is the prevailing cosmological model of the universe's origins. However, some physicists now argue that the Big Bang model leads to too many inconsistencies with empirical evidence. Though cosmologists have made ad-hoc additions to the theory to make the Big Bang fit with our observations, it is time to consider alternatives, including models in which the universe has no beginning.*

The current standard model of cosmology (*Big Bang* or ΛCDM) gives us a representation of a universe whose dynamics is dominated by gravity ([Friedmann equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedmann_equations) derived from Einstein's general relativity) with a finite lifetime, homogeneity on large scales, expansion and a hot initial state, along with other elements necessary to avoid certain inconsistencies with observations (inﬂation, non-baryonic dark matter, dark energy, etc). This prevailing cosmological model has countless problems, but it is *mainstream*, and almost all the scientific and economic efforts of the community of astrophysicists and theoretical physicists dealing with the question are focused on the search for evidence that can confirm it. Other models explaining the cosmos without a *Big Bang* beginning, without an inflationary period, without energy and dark matter, etc do not receive the same attention. If any of them are correct, the cosmos could be very different from the mainstream version we have been taught as dogma.

Among the most developed *non-Big Bang* alternative models, perhaps the hypothesis with the greatest impact has been the *Quasi-steady State Cosmology*, originally called *Steady State*. Its origins date back to the 1940s and was proposed by [Fred Hoyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Hoyle) (1915-2001), and independently by [Hermann Bondi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Bondi) (1919-2005) and [Thomas Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gold) (1920-2004). It was developed and defended for more than sixty years by many other professional researchers, and even today we cannot declare it dead, although its main proponents have passed away or retired, and new generations of cosmologists no longer work with these scenarios.

It is a radically different view, in which, contrary to the *Big Bang* approach, there is no beginning of the Universe but an eternal cycle of matter creation. The Universe is expanding and the homogeneous distribution of matter is being created at a constant rate due to the existence of a supposedly omnipresent C-field of matter creation, rather than a single moment of creation at the *Big Bang*. The newly created matter forms new galaxies that replace those swept away by the expansion.

During the 1950s, both the *Big Bang* and the *Steady-State* ideas found support. Both explained the abundance of the chemical elements. The abundances of the light elements (helium, lithium, deuterium and others) would be explained by the *Steady-State* hypothesis in terms of stellar nucleosynthesis and collision with cosmic rays in the distant past of the Universe, whereas with the *Big Bang* they were formed during the initial stages at the beginning of the Universe. The heavy elements were both of stellar origin.

However, *Steady-State* hypothesis would lose competitiveness in the mid-1960s, because it could not explain certain observational facts. It could not explain why the most distant galaxies were younger. Nor could it explain the excess of radio sources at large distances, or the distribution of quasars. More importantly, it did not explain the cosmic microwave background. This strongly favoured the Big Bang hypothesis.

In [1993-94](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1994MNRAS.267.1007H/abstract), Fred Hoyle, [Geoffrey Burbidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Burbidge) (1925-2010) and [Jayant Narlikar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayant_Narlikar) (1938- ) published a modification of the *Steady-State* model, called *Quasi-Steady-State* model. It consisted of postulating a cyclic oscillatory expansion, and in which the creation of matter is limited to epochs with a minimum size of the Universe. Since the time scale is very long, many generations of stars (and galaxies) evolve and die. With the introduction of the *Quasi-* variation, some of the problems that plagued the original hypothesis were solved. This explained why galaxies are younger at large distances, the problem of radio sources, the distribution of quasars, and the formation of large-scale structure, among others. The microwave background and its blackbody spectrum would be explained as the effect of thermalisation of radiation emitted by late-cycle stars. But the scientific community had already decided which horse was the winner before the end of the race, and ignored the new version of the 1990s model.

Another remarkable model of eternal universe has been the [*plasma cosmology*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cosmology) by the Nobel Prize winner in physics [Hannes Alfvén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannes_Alfvén) (1908-1995), and is still being defended by present-day researchers like [Eric J. Lerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Lerner) (1947- ), author of the remarkable book [*The Big Bang Never Happened*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/100124/the-big-bang-never-happened-by-eric-j-lerner/)(1991)*.* It abandons the dogmas that gravity dominates the dynamics of the Universe and proposes that the cosmos is plasma moved by electromagnetic forces in addition to gravity, rather than gravity alone. According to this hypothesis, the universe has always existed, it is always evolving, and it will continue to exist forever. The inspiration of this model stems from the laboratory, where it is observed that, through electric currents and magnetic fields, filaments like those observed in the large-scale filamentary structure of the universe are created. The formation of galaxies and their dynamics would also be governed by forces and interactions of electromagnetic fields.

Expansion of the universe was admitted in the first version of *plasma cosmology* and was explained by means of the repulsion between matter and antimatter: *fireworks* model, in which a supercluster is repelled by other superclusters; within a supercluster each cluster is repelled by other clusters; and within a given cluster each galaxy is repelled by the other galaxies, and so on, obeying a distribution of matter and antimatter. However, the energy derived from the annihilation of matter and antimatter would produce a background radiation of X-ray and γ-ray, which is problematic in the comparison with the observations of these backgrounds.

Instead of expansion caused by matter-antimatter repulsion, recently some proponents of plasma cosmology have stated that there is no expansion, that the universe is static, and that the redshift of the galaxies would be explained by some kind of [*tired light*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tired_light) effect (loss of energy of photons due to some interaction along their path) of the interaction of photons with electrons in the plasma.

With regard to the cosmic microwave background radiation, it was explained in terms of absorption and re-emission of radiation produced by stars. It is similar to the mechanism proposed by *Quasi-Steady-State Cosmology*, but here the thermalization is due to interaction with electrons. The interaction of photons and electrons would produce a loss of direction in the path of the light, giving rise to isotropic radiation.

Many other models of an eternal universe would be proposed. Some of them are cyclic universes of periodic *Big Bangs,* like [Roger Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Penrose) (1931- )’s [*Conformal Cyclic Cosmology*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conformal_cyclic_cosmology), for instance, or the endless sequence of epochs that starts with a *Big Bang* singularity and ends in a *Big Crunch* (collapse of the universe due to an excess of mass-energy of the critical density in a closed universe). Another model with infinite cycles is the [*Dynamic Universe*](https://www.physicsfoundations.org/dynamic-universe) by [Tuomo Suntola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuomo_Suntola) (1943- ), where the space is represented as a spherically closed whole: space as the 3D surface of a 4D hypersphere and a contraction-expansion cycle, though this model also allows a finite number of cycles.

At the farthest extreme of alternative models with respect to the standard cosmology, we have proposals of *static* universe that contradict the main interpretation upon which standard cosmology was created from the 1920s onwards: expansion of the universe and the interpretation of redshifts as cosmological. Some versions of plasma and hypersphere cosmologies figure among these models, but there exist plenty of other models that are characterized by the lack of an origin of time (an eternal universe), and lack of expansion, in some cases the space even being infinite and Euclidean. The redshift of galaxies given by Hubble-Lemaître's law would be due to some mechanism different from the expansion or Doppler effect, mainly a *tired light* effect or others. Static models are usually rejected by most cosmologists.  However, from a purely theoretical point of view, the representation of the Cosmos as static is not excluded. Both expanding and static spaces are possible for the description of the universe.

All of these alternative models have problems to fit the observed reality. But the *Big Bang* model has also found and continues to find many major inconsistencies with the observations, although it corrects them *ad hoc*, patching up the model here and there in the style of the Ptolemaic [deferents and epicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deferent_and_epicycle): non-baryonic dark matter, dark energy, inflation, etc. However, when predictive failures occur in competing hypotheses contrary to the *mainstream*, they are shown to be definitively proven false.

That’s the way the life is. One cannot make a silk purse out of a pig’s ear. We do not know whether the universe had a beginning or is eternal, but we do know that cosmology is human, not divine or the fruit of pure rational minds, and is subject to the earthly dynamics of games of power and double standards.
